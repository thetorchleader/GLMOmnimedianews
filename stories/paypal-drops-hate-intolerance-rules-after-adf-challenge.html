PayPal Drops ‘Hate’ and ‘Intolerance’ Rules After Religious Liberty Challenge

By GLM Omnimedia News November 8, 2026 Source: Christian Post

PayPal has removed the words “hate” and “intolerance” from its rules on banned transactions, after the Christian legal group Alliance Defending Freedom raised objections with a federal banking regulator. The change narrows what the payment company can treat as a reason to cut off a customer’s account.

Under PayPal’s old rule, the company barred transactions involving “the promotion of hate, violence, racial or other forms of intolerance that is discriminatory,” according to ADF. The new wording limits the ban to discrimination against traits “protected under applicable law.” In other words, the rule now turns on what the law actually forbids, not on the company’s own judgment about a customer’s views.

PayPal is also narrowing its rule against “misleading information.” Under the change, that rule will cover only statements about PayPal transactions themselves. What a customer says on other subjects will fall outside it.

The company updated its Acceptable Use Policy this week, after setting out the changes in a September 15 letter to the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation. It also posted a new statement on its frequently asked questions page saying it bases account decisions “on account activity, not a customer’s religion, race, disability, gender, sexual orientation, national origin, political affiliation, or any characteristic protected under applicable law.”

ADF filed a comment with the FDIC in July, warning that PayPal’s terms allowed the company to remove users for “hate,” “intolerance,” and “misleading information,” among other subjective standards. The group said such terms could amount to “politicized or unlawful debanking” under Executive Order 14331, signed by President Donald Trump, which states that Americans should not lose access to financial services because of beliefs protected by the Constitution or by statute.

The letter to the FDIC set out evidence that PayPal had closed or frozen accounts under its earlier terms for reasons that appeared tied to what customers lawfully believed or said about politics. PayPal froze accounts held by the Free Speech Union and its founder in 2022. That same year it froze accounts held by Moms for Liberty and restored them only after public criticism.

PayPal has applied to the FDIC to insure deposits at PayPal Bank, an industrial bank it plans to set up in Utah. ADF withdrew its objection to that application after PayPal notified the FDIC of the policy changes, saying it understood the revised policy to apply only to unlawful discrimination and to leave lawful religious practice and speech untouched.

Jeremy Tedesco, an ADF senior counsel and senior vice president of its Counter-Censorship Task Force, said no American should have to worry about losing a bank or payment service over their faith or politics. “PayPal has a history of mixing business with politics, and its customers pay the price when they are punished for holding views the company dislikes,” he said. He called PayPal’s commitments an important step and said ADF would monitor to ensure the changes are carried out and enforced fairly, urging other banks and financial firms to drop terms such as “hate” and “misinformation” and replace them with clear, objective rules.

Caleb Dalton, another ADF senior counsel, praised PayPal for the changes, saying they advance the company’s “commitment to fair, consistent and objective account management.” ADF said most of the largest U.S. banks have changed their policies along the same lines in recent years, pointing to a recent report by the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency that it said showed lenders and other financial firms relying on vague policies to treat lawful activities and opinions unfairly, particularly religious views.

The wider push follows President Trump’s August 7, 2025, executive order, “Guaranteeing Fair Banking for All Americans,” which directs federal banking regulators to remove “reputation risk” from their guidance and to identify institutions that have engaged in politicized or unlawful debanking. In December 2025, the OCC released preliminary findings on the nine largest national banks it supervises, saying those banks had restricted services between 2020 and 2023 to some lawful industries, including firearms, coal mining, and digital assets.

For churches, ministries, and Christian families who depend on payment services to receive donations and pay their bills, the change is a welcome one. A bank or payment company should judge a customer by their conduct, not by their creed. When the rules are vague, the power to enforce them becomes the power to punish belief. Clear, objective standards protect everyone — including the Christian who simply wants to bank, give, and do business in peace.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

No one should have to choose between their faith and their bank account. The Bible teaches that honest weights and just measures belong to the Lord (Proverbs 11:1) — and that standard applies to the marketplace as surely as to the pulpit. When financial firms judge customers by clear, lawful rules instead of vague labels, Christians can give, receive, and do business without fear that their beliefs will cost them their livelihood.
