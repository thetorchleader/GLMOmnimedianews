Josh Howerton: John Piper's 'Seashell Sermon' Wrecked My Life Forever — and Changed a Generation

By GLM Omnimedia News October 28, 2026 Source: ChurchLeaders

While preaching on Sunday, October 25, about the rich young ruler, Texas pastor Josh Howerton pointed his congregation to a sermon that changed his life — and, he said, the direction of his entire generation.

Howerton, senior pastor of Lakepointe Church in the Dallas-Fort Worth area, was recalling Pastor John Piper's message titled "Boast Only in the Cross," preached at the Passion One Day gathering on May 20, 2000. The message has since become known as the "seashell sermon," and ChurchLeaders reports that it moved many listeners, Howerton among them, toward a life of ministry.

Howerton was still in high school when Piper preached the sermon, and he did not hear it until years later, when a college friend handed him a burned CD of the recording. "Those next 42 minutes wrecked my life forever," he told his congregation. "I am doing what I'm doing today because of what I heard."

He listened so often, he said, that he could repeat the sermon word for word from beginning to end. On his third date with his now-wife Jana, he played it for her, telling her plainly, "This is what I'm all about."

On the October 26 episode of his "Live Free" podcast, Howerton returned to the sermon, saying it "perfectly encapsulates" the passage about the rich young ruler. He called it "the most impactful 2 minutes of preaching I've ever seen," writing on social media that it "changed my entire life."

He then played a clip from that rainy day in May 2000, when Piper preached to some 40,000 college students in a field outside Memphis, Tennessee. Piper told the young crowd it was sad that so many young people simply want to be liked and to live out the American dream. "You don't give a rip whether your life counts on this earth for eternity," he said. "That's a tragedy in the making."

Piper then told the crowd about two elderly women from his church, Bethlehem Baptist in Minneapolis, who had recently died in a car accident while serving as missionaries in Cameroon. That, he said, was not a tragedy. A tragedy, he told them, was the story he had read in Reader's Digest about a middle-aged couple who retired early to enjoy a carefree life in Florida, spending their days boating and collecting seashells.

Twenty-six years later, the contrast still preaches: a life poured out for Christ on the mission field is not wasted, while a life spent only on comfort and pleasure — however pleasant — is the true loss. Howerton's testimony is a reminder that one faithful sermon, heard at the right moment, can redirect a whole life toward eternity.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

The rich young ruler walked away sorrowful because he loved his possessions more than Christ (Mark 10:22), but the two missionaries who died in Cameroon gave everything and lost nothing. "For what shall it profit a man, if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul?" (Mark 8:36). A life is not measured by how many seashells it collects, but by whether it counts for eternity: "God forbid that I should glory, save in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ" (Galatians 6:14).
