Kenyan Bishop Urges Christians to Bless Those Who Wrong Them

By GLM Omnimedia News November 8, 2026 Source: EWTN News

A Kenyan bishop has called on Christians to bless the very people who have hurt them, pointing to the night Jesus gave the Church the Lord's Supper even as betrayal closed in around Him.

Bishop Mark Kadima Wamukoya of the Diocese of Bungoma delivered the message in his homily during the consecration of St. Mark's Ojamii Catholic Parish church on November 5, according to EWTN News. The bishop reflected on how Christ instituted the Eucharist at the very hour He was betrayed.

"Jesus Christ instituted the Eucharist at the time he was betrayed. Despite the pain, suffering, and betrayal, he gave us a lasting blessing," Kadima said.

The bishop urged the congregation to follow that example in their own relationships. "This teaches us that even when someone wrongs us, we should still bless them. Even when we are hurt or mistreated, we should come to church, pray, and ask God to bless others," he said. "This is a place where we should leave our differences behind and say, 'May everyone be blessed.'"

Kadima stressed that the church is a house of prayer and of the sacraments, especially the Eucharist, and he encouraged the faithful to keep coming to worship even when their homes and families are going through hard times. "Coming to church does not mean that everything in our lives is perfect. Our families, marriages, and children may be facing challenges, but we still come to church to pray and ask God to bless everyone," he said.

Turning to the promises believers make before God, the bishop called on Christians to take their commitments seriously, including the vows made at priestly ordination and in the sacrament of marriage. "The Church is a place where we come to make promises and honor them," he said. "When we say yes at the altar, that yes must mean something."

Recalling his own ordination, Kadima said he was brought before the altar and instructed to lie down, a reminder that a commitment once made must be kept. "We need the Church because we are a people of promises. Our lives as God's people cannot be built without promises and their fulfillment. When we say, 'I accept' or 'I promise,' we must mean it," he said.

The bishop also called on Catholics to respect rules and regulations in their daily lives, linking obedience to service, good conduct, and respectful speech. "When we come to church, we are reminded to become people of service and good conduct. We must cultivate a culture of obedience and respect for rules, whether in the Church, at the table, at the altar, or in the way we communicate with one another," he said.

The story was first published by ACI Africa, the sister service of EWTN News in Africa, and adapted by EWTN News.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

Blessing those who wrong us is not weakness; it is obedience to Christ, who gave His body and blood on the very night He was betrayed. "Bless them which persecute you: bless, and curse not" (Romans 12:14, KJV). When we pray for those who hurt us, we leave our differences at the door of the church and let God be the judge.
