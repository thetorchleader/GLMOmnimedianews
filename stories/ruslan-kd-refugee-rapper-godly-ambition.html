Ambition Isn't the Enemy: How a Former Refugee Turned Rapper Is Calling Christians to Dream Big for God

By GLM Omnimedia News October 31, 2026 Source: CBN News

Ruslan KD arrived in the United States as a six-year-old refugee, his Armenian family fleeing ethnic violence in Baku, Azerbaijan, in the final years of the Soviet Union. Decades later, he is reaching millions online — first as a hip-hop artist, and now as a Christian YouTuber, podcaster, and author — with a message many believers rarely hear from the pulpit: ambition itself is not the enemy.

That is the case Ruslan makes in his book, Godly Ambition: Unlocking the Full Potential of Your Time, Talent, and Treasure, published by WaterBrook in September 2025, according to CBN News. The book argues that the drive to build, create, and achieve becomes holy when it is anchored in identity in Christ and aimed at serving others rather than feeding the ego.

Ruslan's own life gives the argument its weight. He grew up in a single-parent home after his parents split following the family's arrival in America, and he has spoken openly about early trouble, including an arrest at age eleven. In his late teens he encountered Christ through a local church, and apologetics — the reasoned defense of the faith — became his doorway into a serious walk with God.

From 2015 to 2020 he worked as a full-time independent hip-hop artist. When the pandemic disrupted live music, he pivoted to YouTube, where cultural commentary, interviews, and livestreams built an audience of more than a million followers. The platform, he has said, lets one voice reach crowds that once would have required a production company and an enormous budget.

At the heart of Godly Ambition is a distinction: selfish ambition chases applause, wealth, and status for their own sake, while godly ambition treats time, talent, and treasure as stewardship — gifts entrusted by God to be invested for His kingdom. The book walks readers through clarifying their identity and purpose in Christ, addressing old wounds, removing distractions, and building habits that keep big goals from swallowing the soul.

Ruslan is also frank that ambition without community goes wrong. He stresses placing oneself under the authority of a local church, staying accountable to trusted leaders, and keeping a good team close — guardrails he presents as essential for anyone building a public platform in the social-media age.

The message lands in a culture that often pulls believers in two opposite directions: either baptizing hustle culture wholesale, or treating any desire to achieve as suspect. Ruslan's answer is a middle path with deep biblical roots — work hard, dream big, and measure success not by the world's metrics but by faithfulness to the calling God has given.

For Christians who have felt guilty about wanting to do something significant with their lives, the refugee-turned-rapper's story offers a different frame: the same drive that once led him astray, redeemed and redirected, is now the engine of a ministry reaching people he could never have met from a stage alone.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

Colossians 3:23 says, "And whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men." Ambition becomes godly when it is offered back to God: the question is never whether we will dream big, but whether our dreams serve His kingdom or only ourselves.
