The BCNN1 Torch Leader News Brief — Sunday, November 8, 2026

By GLM Omnimedia News November 8, 2026 Source: BCNN1 (multiple sources)

Seven stories for Sunday evening, November 8, 2026 — from the persecuted church overseas to the church here at home — gathered for BCNN1 readers coming out of church today. Each story is reported as its source reported it; pray where prayer is asked for.

ETHIOPIA: 60 ORTHODOX CHRISTIANS KILLED IN OROMIA ATTACKS

At least 60 Orthodox Christians were killed in separate attacks on October 20 and 24 in Ethiopia's Oromia region, church officials reported. The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church said 14 people were killed on October 20 in Gora Silingo, in the Merti district, and 46 more on October 24 in the villages of Tereta, Chole, and Birbo, in the Shirka district — with more than 20 people seriously injured and 20 homes burned down. The church's holy synod called the killings "extremely tragic cruelty," said they violated constitutional protections for life and religious freedom, and called on the government to protect civilians and bring those responsible to justice. The Oromia regional government has pledged to pursue those responsible; there was no immediate claim of responsibility for the attacks. (Source: Al Jazeera)

NIGERIA: TWO CHRISTIANS KILLED, ABOUT 18 SEIZED IN PRE-DAWN KADUNA RAID

Around 12:30 a.m. on Wednesday, November 4, armed attackers laid siege to Akwando village in the Kachia area of southern Kaduna State, Nigeria, killing two Christians and abducting about 18 people. Reuben Buhari, co-founder of the Resilience Aid Dialogue Initiative, said most of those taken are married women and children from one extended Christian family — 15 females and three males, including infants between four months and one year old. Community reports named a church choirmaster among the two men killed, and a community association released a list of names of the abducted. Nigerian authorities and police had offered no public response to the raid in the reports. (Sources: CathoNews; community reports carried by Nigerian outlets)

PERSECUTED CHURCH: NIGERIA ACCOUNTS FOR NEARLY THREE OF EVERY FOUR CHRISTIANS KILLED FOR THEIR FAITH

As churches mark the season of the International Day of Prayer for the Persecuted Church, a Christian Post essay by Brad Brandon, founder of Across Nigeria, cites Open Doors' 2026 World Watch List: 4,849 Christians were killed worldwide for faith-related reasons in the latest reporting period, and 3,490 of them — nearly three-quarters — were killed in Nigeria alone. Open Doors also recorded 2,293 Christians abducted in Nigeria in the same period, and Brandon recalled the June 2025 attack on the Christian farming community of Yelwata in Benue State, where 258 people, mostly women and children, were killed. His plea to Western churches: the persecuted church needs more than one day of prayer a year. (Source: The Christian Post)

CALVARY CHAPEL COSTA MESA ANNOUNCES IT ORDAINS WOMEN PASTORS; FELLOW PASTORS ANSWER

Calvary Chapel Costa Mesa in California — once the flagship church of the Calvary Chapel movement founded by Chuck Smith — announced during its November 1 service that it "believes and practices the ordination of both men and women to pastoral ministry," with Pastor Char Brodersen reading a statement citing the priesthood of all believers. Pastors in the movement answered publicly against the decision: the Calvary Chapel Association's Heartland Regional Team said it sees no sound biblical basis for ordaining women as elders and pastors and that the practice conflicts with the association's distinctives. Calvary Chapel Liberty in Missouri likewise rejected the ordination of women as elders or pastors in a signed statement, and another Calvary Chapel pastor wrote that his church had cut ties with Costa Mesa after Smith's death in 2013. (Source: The Christian Post)

WEST VIRGINIA'S HIGHEST COURT BLOCKS LAWSUIT BY 33 CHURCHES SEEKING TO LEAVE THE UNITED METHODIST CHURCH

The Supreme Court of Appeals of West Virginia ruled on November 4 that a lawsuit by 33 congregations seeking to leave the United Methodist Church with their property cannot go forward in civil court. The churches sued after the denomination's 2024 General Conference removed its bans on gay clergy and same-sex marriage, saying conference officials misled them into missing a deadline to withdraw with property. Writing for the court, Justice Charles S. Trump IV said deciding the dispute would require civil courts to interpret church law — forbidden by the First Amendment to the U.S. Constitution and by West Virginia's constitution — and the court stopped a lower court from reopening the case, saying the matter belongs to the church's own highest tribunal, the Judicial Council. (Sources: The Christian Post; UM News; the court's signed opinion)

CONGRESS RETURNS MONDAY FACING A DECEMBER 11 FUNDING DEADLINE

Lawmakers return to Capitol Hill on Monday, November 9, for a lame-duck session with the government's temporary funding set to run out on December 11. The temporary measure Congress approved earlier this year kept the government running through Election Day, and reporters covering the session say a shutdown before the holidays is possible if no spending agreement is reached. Also unfinished: the remaining spending bills for fiscal year 2027, the National Defense Authorization Act, and the Farm Bill. The new Congress elected on November 3 is scheduled to be seated in the first days of January 2027. (Sources: Gray DC reporting carried by WCAX, WIS, and other stations; The Center Square)

A HOPEFUL NOTE: YOUVERSION'S NEW "BIBLE LOOP" APP FOR TEENAGERS PASSES ONE MILLION INSTALLS

YouVersion, maker of the Bible App, has launched Bible Loop, a free app built with the ministry OneHope to help teenagers build a daily habit in God's Word. The app offers a verse memorization game with an anonymous leaderboard, Daily Check-Ins that match devotional content to how a teen is feeling, a Verse of the Day, and Daily Prayers, with Bible text in more than 50 versions across 10 languages. It is available on Android and iOS in English, Spanish, Portuguese, and French. YouVersion founder Bobby Gruenewald told The Christian Post the app, first tested in India, has already been installed more than one million times worldwide. (Sources: The Christian Post; YouVersion)
