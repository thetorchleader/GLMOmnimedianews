Federal Judge Limits Agriculture Secretary Brooke Rollins' Religious Emails to USDA Staff

By GLM Omnimedia News November 23, 2026 Source: Charisma News

United States Agriculture Secretary Brooke Rollins has been told by a federal judge to limit how she shares her Christian faith in official messages to the employees of her department. The ruling follows two holiday emails in which Rollins spoke openly about Jesus Christ, and it raises fresh questions about where the line falls between a public official's personal faith and the power of her office.

On Easter Sunday, Rollins sent an email to U.S. Department of Agriculture employees declaring, "Jesus has been raised from the dead." Her April 5 Easter message called Jesus "our risen Lord" and included an illustration of the stone rolled away from the tomb. Months earlier, on December 23, 2025, she sent a Christmas video in which she called Jesus "our Savior" and said He "came to free us from our sins and open the door to eternal life."

Last Thursday, U.S. District Judge Trina Thompson found that those messages likely violated the First Amendment's Establishment Clause, and she issued a preliminary injunction limiting how Rollins may speak of her faith in official communications with employees. The judge pointed to three things: Rollins' position of authority over the staff, her use of an official government email account, and language that implied employees shared her faith — a combination, the judge found, that created pressure to conform.

The case was brought in May by the National Federation of Federal Employees and six USDA employees. They argued that staff members could not opt out of department-wide emails, and that Rollins had not sent comparable holiday greetings for holidays observed by other faiths.

Rollins pushed back publicly on Friday, writing on X, "So the Biden White House could recognize Transgender Day of Visibility on Easter Sunday, but sharing a Christian Easter message with USDA employees crosses the line?" She followed the question with the words of Jesus from Matthew 5 about letting our light shine before others.

The injunction does not silence Rollins entirely. Under the order, she must follow existing USDA guidance that bars officials from using their authority to pressure employees, but she may still discuss her faith with interested colleagues and invite them to religious services. A USDA spokesperson said the department was considering an appeal.

Rollins herself has made clear she does not intend to stop speaking of her faith. In remarks cited in a September court filing and quoted in Judge Thompson's ruling, she said, "I have been sued a couple of times for, they say, talking about Jesus too much, but I'll never stop talking about Jesus."

The ruling is preliminary, not final: the judge found the messages likely violated the Constitution, and the case continues. Whatever the courts ultimately decide, the dispute is a reminder that believers in public office walk a careful road — called to be faithful witnesses for Christ, and called at the same time to respect the trust placed in them over people of many faiths and of none.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

Scripture tells us to let our light shine before men so that they may see our good works and glorify our Father in heaven (Matthew 5:16), and it also calls those in authority to lead with humility and respect. Christians in public life are to speak the truth boldly and love their neighbors well — including neighbors who do not share their faith — trusting God, not government power, to draw hearts to Christ.
