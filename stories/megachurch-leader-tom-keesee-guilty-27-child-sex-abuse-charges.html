Ohio Megachurch Leader Tom Keesee Convicted on 27 Child Sex Abuse Counts

By GLM Omnimedia News November 9, 2026 Source: Protestia

Tom Keesee, the 36-year-old son of the founders and senior pastors of Faith Life Church in New Albany, Ohio, has been found guilty of 27 felony counts related to the sexual abuse of three girls, according to reporting by Protestia.

The convictions include 11 counts of rape of a child under 13, 11 counts of gross sexual imposition, and 5 counts of unlawful sexual conduct with a minor. Keesee was employed at the 3,000-member megachurch as its chief media officer when police arrested him, after allegations of sexual abuse were brought against him, including by his own sister.

During the trial, it was revealed that Keesee had at least three victims, all of whom were members of the church and were between the ages of 5 and 15 when the abuse began. In some cases, the abuse lasted for more than five years.

Keesee remained unemotional as the verdict was read. He is set to be sentenced on January 8, where he faces life in prison without the possibility of parole. He remains free on bond until then, but must wear an ankle bracelet and has no driver's license or passport.

Separate from the counts on which he was convicted, Keesee has also been accused of sexually assaulting his ex-wife's sister, and of engaging in a sexual relationship with his current wife while she was still a minor. Those accusations have not resulted in convictions as reported, and they must not be confused with the 27 counts on which a jury found him guilty.

Before the indictment, and after their daughter came forward with allegations against her brother, pastors Gary and Drenda Keesee defended their son, telling church members during a service that there was "another side of the story" and that "truth will prevail." During the trial, multiple victims testified under oath that Drenda Keesee was aware of her son's abuse but failed to notify authorities, and Keesee's older brother Tim also testified that their parents knew of the abuse years before it came to light. That testimony is not itself a finding against the parents, and no charges against them have been reported.

This is a grievous story, and it ought to be reported plainly. A church is meant to be a place where children are safe, where the vulnerable are protected, and where wrongdoing is brought into the light. When leaders fail in that duty, the harm reaches far beyond one family or one congregation. Churches must have clear policies for protecting children, for reporting suspected abuse to the proper authorities at once, and for standing with victims rather than with reputation.

The law has now spoken in this case, and sentencing will follow in January. The deeper work for the Church remains: to guard the little ones entrusted to our care, to tell the truth, and to make sure no child suffers in silence behind a church door.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

Scripture is blunt about the protection of children: "Whoso shall offend one of these little ones which believe in me, it were better for him that a millstone were hanged about his neck, and that he were drowned in the depth of the sea" (Matthew 18:6). The Church must defend the fatherless and do justice to the afflicted and needy (Psalm 82:3) — and that begins with believing children, reporting abuse, and refusing to hide sin for the sake of a ministry's name.
