Churches Burned, Priests Attacked: France Records More Than 840 Anti-Christian Attacks in a Year

By GLM Omnimedia News November 16, 2026 Source: Charisma News

France, once called the eldest daughter of the church, now reports more than two attacks on Christians every day, according to the French government. CBN News traveled to France to see what is left of French Christianity as the country is rocked by repeated violence, with political protesters taking to the streets for a fifth straight weekend.

France's interior ministry says religiously motivated attacks are on the rise, most of them against Jews and Christians. Last year the government counted more than 840 anti-Christian attacks, most of them against churches — arson, vandalism, and desecration. Attacks on people jumped by more than 70 percent, and attacks on clergy quadrupled.

Israel's Ambassador to France, Joshua Zarka, said the Jewish community absorbs most of the anti-religious hate crimes, but added that Jews are not the only ones suffering. “We're not the only ones actually suffering from racial attacks here. People are burning churches, priests are being murdered,” he said.

The murder of Father Jacques Hamel in 2016 remains a defining wound. Two men entered a church just outside Rouen and slit the priest's throat at the altar while he was giving Mass. Since then, thousands of attacks on Christians have been recorded — about a thousand such attacks a year are being recorded now, according to the report. Some people blame Muslim immigration for the violence, but the two men who murdered Father Hamel were French citizens, and the report points to online radicalization as the deeper problem at the heart of the unrest.

Pastor Dorothée Rajiah leads the Paris Christian Centre on the edge of the city, in an area with a strong radical Islamic presence. When the church told the authorities there would be a cross on the building, she said, officials asked the church to have extra security and bulletproof windows.

More than 200 miles south, in Limoges, Pastor Tim Knickerbocker has served for 55 years. The pressure he experiences is not like the firebombs elsewhere, he said, but “something that's actually harder to see.” “It's like a coldness. The whole society is cold in the sense of legislation. We're free to do lots of things you can't even do in the USA, but we feel like under a cloud,” he said. Evangelicals of all kinds together make up about 0.6 percent of France's population, he noted — by the same measure, about 5 percent of communist China is Christian.

Yet both pastors see hope. New people walk into the Christian Centre every week, Pastor Rajiah said, many of them young, many of them immigrants. “The hardness of life really brings people to Jesus,” she said. “So it is bad that brings a good, because people realize that they cannot make it without God, and they search for Jesus.” She believes the nation that built the cathedrals will survive only by coming back to its roots — to Christianity, and to Jesus. France elects a new president in April, but Christians there say they are putting their hope a little higher.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

“Blessed are they which are persecuted for righteousness' sake: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven” (Matthew 5:10). Pray for the Christians of France — for the pastors who preach under a cloud, for the congregations that gather behind extra security, and for the young people walking through the doors searching for Jesus. Persecution has never stopped the Gospel; again and again in history, it has spread it.
