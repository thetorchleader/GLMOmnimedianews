After the Midterms, Pro-Life Leaders Face a Sober Map — and a Renewed Call to Defend Life

By GLM Omnimedia News November 6, 2026 Source: Christianity Today

The pro-life movement woke up the morning after the midterm elections to a sobering map. In Tuesday's voting, the cause of protecting the unborn lost ground in three states and held it in one — and leaders on both sides of the question are now asking what comes next.

According to Christianity Today, voters in Virginia and Nevada approved measures adding a right to abortion to their state constitutions, while Missouri voters rejected a ballot measure that would have restricted abortion. Idaho was the lone victory for the pro-life cause: voters there turned down a measure that would have repealed the state's near-total ban on abortion.

The results are a continuation of the state-by-state battles that have followed the Supreme Court's 2022 Dobbs decision, which overturned Roe v. Wade and returned the question of abortion to the states. Since then, voters in more than ten states have backed measures protecting or expanding abortion access.

In Virginia, the new constitutional amendment grants unrestricted access to abortion during the first two trimesters of pregnancy, allows government regulation during the third trimester, and permits abortion throughout pregnancy when a doctor deems it medically necessary to protect the life and health of the mother. The amendment also adds a constitutional right to contraception and fertility care. In Nevada, more than 60 percent of voters supported a measure granting a constitutional right to abortion until fetal viability — the point at which a baby could survive outside the womb, generally around 24 to 26 weeks of pregnancy, according to the Guttmacher Institute. Nevada requires two votes to amend its constitution, and the question had previously been approved in 2024.

In Missouri, voters rejected a measure that would have restricted abortions except in cases of rape, incest, or fetal anomaly. Voters there had approved a measure protecting abortion rights in 2024, and the Republican-led legislature had hoped to reverse that decision with this year's ballot question. With its defeat, abortion remains legal in Missouri until fetal viability.

Idaho's outcome was different. The state currently bans nearly all abortions, with exceptions only for rape or incest in the first trimester, for ectopic or molar pregnancies, and when the mother's life is at stake. Voters rejected a measure that would have lifted the ban and allowed abortion until fetal viability.

Sarah Zagorski, a spokesperson for Americans United for Life, told Christianity Today the losses show the movement still has far to go. "While we are heartened by the Idaho measure's defeat, the devastating losses at the ballot box in Missouri, Nevada, and Virginia show that we still have a long way to go in changing hearts and shifting culture toward life," she said. She urged stronger voter education and grassroots work, saying voters cannot make informed decisions on ballot measures without understanding what the language would change.

Marjorie Dannenfelser, president of Susan B. Anthony Pro-Life America, pointed part of the blame at Republican leaders, saying the party must stop running from the pro-life fight and give voters a reason to fight for it. She praised pro-life advocates in Idaho, calling the win there a hard-fought example for pro-life voters, candidates, and elected officials across the country.

With Democrats now controlling both the U.S. House and the Senate, pro-life advocates expect harder fights ahead — and they note their own struggles with the second Trump administration, which has expanded access to mail-order abortion drugs. "It makes our work more urgent, not less," Zagorski said. "Our immediate focus must be defending decades of hard-won pro-life protections, especially the Hyde Amendment and other safeguards against taxpayer-funded abortion."

For Christians, the message of this election is plain: laws rise and fall with the winds of politics, but the worth of every unborn child is settled by God, not by a ballot. The Church's calling does not change with the returns — to speak clearly for life, to stand beside mothers in crisis, and to keep praying and working until the culture itself treasures the child in the womb.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

Scripture teaches that God knits each child together in the womb: "Before I formed thee in the belly I knew thee" (Jeremiah 1:5). Elections may shift the law, but they cannot change the value God places on every life — and the Church's duty to defend the unborn and care for mothers remains, whatever the vote.
