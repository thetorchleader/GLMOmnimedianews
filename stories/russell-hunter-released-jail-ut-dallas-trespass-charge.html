Pro-Life Activist Russell Hunter Freed on Bond After Arrest at UT Dallas; Lawsuit May Follow

By GLM Omnimedia News November 21, 2026 Source: Protestia

Russell Hunter, founder of the pro-life group Abolitionist Rising, was released from jail on Thursday afternoon after posting bond, following his arrest at the University of Texas at Dallas, according to Protestia.

Hunter had been speaking with students on campus about abortion, holding a sign and two cameras, when some students complained about his message. Campus officials then asked him to leave, and university police arrested him and charged him with criminal trespass.

His attorney, CJ Grisham of GFA Law, a firm that handles civil rights cases, said Hunter had been standing at the Plinth, the open plaza in the middle of campus where students gather, hold events, and protest each week. According to Grisham, Hunter had no bullhorn, blocked no sidewalk, and chased no one down. Students walked up to him and stayed because they wanted to talk, and the conversations went on for more than two hours in a civil and productive way.

Grisham said that not one university employee or officer spoke to Hunter during those two hours. Then, he said, Tricia Losavio, the university's associate dean of health and wellness initiatives, walked up and told him he had to leave. Her explanation, captured on video, was that UT Dallas is "not an open campus anymore" and that students had complained they "didn't like what was being shown."

When campus police arrived, Grisham said, Hunter asked what law or policy he was breaking. He received no answer, he said, and less than two minutes later he was in handcuffs, charged with criminal trespass.

Abolitionist Rising said it plans to fight what it called unjust charges, along with what it described as a long pattern of disrespect for the First Amendment coming out of Texas. Grisham's firm said it will not only defend Hunter but will "hold UT Dallas accountable," raising the prospect of a lawsuit against the university over the arrest.

The legal team laid out its case in a public statement, arguing that removing a speaker because listeners object to his message is content-based punishment, and that the Supreme Court has rejected that kind of "heckler's veto" for decades. The statement cited several Supreme Court decisions, including Forsyth County v. Nationalist Movement and Reed v. Town of Gilbert, and noted that a federal court has already preliminarily blocked parts of the University of Texas System's campus speech policies because they likely violate the First Amendment.

The Protestia report did not include a response from the University of Texas at Dallas. The trespass charge against Hunter remains pending, and no court has ruled on whether the arrest was lawful. A charge is an accusation, not a finding of guilt, and the case will now work its way through the legal process.

Whatever the outcome in court, the episode raises a question every believer should care about: does a public university have the right to remove a peaceful speaker from a public plaza simply because some students dislike his message? The First Amendment exists precisely to protect speech that others find unwelcome, and that protection matters most when the message is about the lives of the unborn.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

Scripture commands us to "speak up for those who cannot speak for themselves" (Proverbs 31:8). Hunter was speaking for the unborn on a public campus, and believers should pray that truth is not silenced simply because some find it uncomfortable. "The truth shall make you free" (John 8:32) — and a free people must be free to speak it.
