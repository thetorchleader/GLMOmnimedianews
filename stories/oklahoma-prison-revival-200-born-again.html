200 Men Give Their Lives to Christ as Revival Sweeps Oklahoma Prison

By GLM Omnimedia News November 17, 2026 Source: CBN News

Two hundred men gave their lives to Jesus Christ at an Oklahoma prison this week, and 47 of them were baptized on the spot, according to the prison ministry God Behind Bars. Three hundred incarcerated men had gathered to seek God when the service turned into what the ministry described as a sweeping move of revival behind bars.

God Behind Bars reported men weeping, lifting their hands, and crying out to Jesus as they encountered what the ministry called the life-changing power of Christ. The ministry said revival is not limited to church buildings, and that God is raising up men inside prisons who are surrendering their past and becoming new creations in Him.

Ministers who were on site said the response was not a passing emotional moment. They reported genuine salvations in large numbers, not the temporary jailhouse conversions that prison chaplains sometimes see fade after the service ends. The ministry added that prison walls cannot hold back what God is doing in the hearts of the men inside.

The Oklahoma gathering is the latest in a string of prison revivals CBN News has reported over the past few years. In 2022, God Behind Bars reported more than 450 inmates baptized, up from 90 the year before. In 2023, the ministry reported 130 new baptisms at one federal prison for women, and in 2024 another 140 women were baptized, while 300 men and women gave their lives to Christ at Louisiana State Penitentiary.

The pattern continued into 2025. Four hundred inmates were born again during a Victory Christian Center outreach at an Oklahoma prison, 170 inmates were baptized at Sumner County Jail in Tennessee, and 100 men chose Christ in a Mississippi prison. Smaller events in correctional facilities across the country, the report noted, add up to large numbers when counted together.

CBN News has also produced a documentary, “Revival Generation,” about revival among young people across the United States, pointing to a wider turning toward faith that the Oklahoma prison service appears to be part of. The specific Oklahoma prison where this week's service took place was not named in the report, and no individual inmate was named.

For the men who stood in that prison chapel with their hands lifted, the night was not about a program or a crowd. It was about a Savior who goes where the lost are — even behind bars — and makes all things new.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

“Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new” (2 Corinthians 5:17). No wall, no cell, and no past is too strong for the Gospel. Pray for the 200 men who came to Christ in Oklahoma — that they would be discipled, rooted in the Word, and kept in the faith long after the service is over.
