Voters Split on Girls' Sports, Transgender Surgeries for Minors, and Abortion in State Ballot Measures

By GLM Omnimedia News November 7, 2026 Source: CBN News

Voters in more than half a dozen states decided ballot measures on Tuesday covering girls' sports, medical procedures for minors, and abortion — and the results cut across party lines. Even as Democrats made gains in Congress, voters in several states, including some deeply Democratic ones, backed measures limiting transgender participation in girls' sports and surgeries for minors, according to CBN News.

Five states weighed measures on girls' sports or medical treatment for children with gender dysphoria. Arizona voters approved an expansion of the state's existing ban on transgender athletes competing in girls' sports. In Nebraska, voters overwhelmingly approved Initiative Measure 442, the "Fairness for Girls" initiative, to protect female athletes.

The most striking results came in two states that usually vote Democratic. In Washington state, voters passed Initiative 638, which bars students who were born male from joining female teams. In Colorado, more than 52 percent of voters approved a measure requiring students to play only on teams matching their biological sex — and by the same margin approved Proposition 135, which prohibits the medical industry from surgically altering the gender of minors. The Colorado measure does not restrict puberty blockers.

Missouri went the other way on that question: voters rejected Amendment 3, which would have written restrictions on gender-altering medical procedures for minors into the state constitution.

On abortion, Idaho voters defeated a measure that would have permitted abortion until fetal viability. But abortion-rights supporters won in Missouri, Nevada, and Virginia. In Missouri, voters defeated a proposed constitutional amendment that would have restored restrictions on abortion; CBN News reported that national progressive groups heavily outspent pro-life groups in that campaign.

Nevada voters approved an amendment creating a fundamental right to abortion until fetal viability — a term the amendment leaves officially undefined. Virginia voters approved three constitutional amendments: enshrining abortion, same-sex marriage, and the restoration of voting rights for people convicted of felonies. All three were already legal in Virginia.

Opponents of the Virginia measures warned the language reaches further than current law. According to CBN's report, opponents say the abortion amendment would allow abortions until birth, permit minors to obtain abortions without their parents' knowledge, and could open the door to sex-change procedures for minors because of added gender language. Opponents of the marriage amendment say it creates two separate classifications — birth sex and chosen gender — that could extend transgender rights into private spaces and sports. These are claims by opponents, not established findings, and the measures' supporters dispute them.

For Christians, the mixed results are a reminder that elections settle laws, not hearts. Voters in some of the most liberal states in the country drew a line at children — their bodies, their sports, and their innocence. At the same time, the votes in Missouri, Nevada, and Virginia show how much work remains to persuade neighbors that every unborn child bears the image of God. The task now is the same one the church has always had: to speak truth with love, to pray for leaders and voters alike, and to come alongside mothers, fathers, and children with real help and hope.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

Scripture teaches that God forms each person with purpose: "For thou hast possessed my reins: thou hast covered me in my mother's womb. I will praise thee; for I am fearfully and wonderfully made" (Psalm 139:13-14, KJV). Whatever the ballot box decides, Christians are called to defend children and the unborn, to speak truth with love, and to pray for a nation that still needs the Gospel.
