Operation Christmas Child Aims for 12.8 Million Shoeboxes, With a First-Ever Delivery to Venezuela

By GLM Omnimedia News November 24, 2026 Source: Christian Post

Operation Christmas Child has wrapped up its annual collection week, and the ministry of Samaritan's Purse is aiming to deliver 12.8 million shoebox gifts to children around the world in 2026. The shoeboxes, packed by families and churches, carry toys, school supplies, and other small gifts — and local churches use the deliveries as a chance to share the Gospel with the children who receive them.

For Franklin Graham, president and CEO of Samaritan's Purse, the power of a shoebox is best told through the children whose lives it has touched. He told The Christian Post about a young boy in a refugee camp who had lost his parents in the war in Bosnia. The boy did not want a Christmas gift. He wanted a family.

When the boy was encouraged to open the shoebox sent to him, he found a photograph of the couple who had packed it. A letter-writing friendship followed, then a visit. About a year later, the couple adopted him.

"This is a kid who said, 'I don't want a box. I want parents.' Well, God put the parents in the box," Graham said.

Churches that take part in the project receive training from Samaritan's Purse on how to host events for children in their communities, inviting neighborhood children for a gathering with refreshments, a Christmas message, and the chance to receive a shoebox.

This year's campaign also marks a first for the ministry: Samaritan's Purse has received permission to bring shoebox gifts from the United States into Venezuela. The organization plans to airlift 20,000 shoeboxes into the country on December 1, as part of its broader relief work after back-to-back earthquakes left families displaced and in need. Samaritan's Purse has also been running an emergency field hospital in Venezuela, where it has treated more than 14,000 patients, and is building shelters and providing clean water for displaced people.

"I think of the children who have lost their homes and lost family members and so forth," Graham said of the earthquake's devastation. He described the chance to deliver a gift to those children as a way to remind them "that God hasn't forgotten them, that they're important to God."

"That's why I want every child in the world to know that God does love them. He does care," he said.

Last year, Operation Christmas Child collected 12.2 million shoebox gifts around the world, including 10.9 million from the United States. While the national collection week has now closed, donors can still take part by packing a virtual shoebox online, choosing a child's age and gender and the items Samaritan's Purse will pack and ship.

Graham has heard hundreds of stories like these over the years. He told of an 11-year-old boy in Idaho who packed a shoebox with a photograph of himself in a cowboy hat, Wrangler pants, and cowboy boots. The box reached a girl living in a slum area in the Philippines who had never seen a cowboy before. She put his picture beside her bed, and the church that gave her the shoebox shared the Gospel with her. She became a Christian, her parents later came to faith in Christ, and her father eventually became a pastor at the church. Years later, she and the young man from Idaho met, fell in love, and married — and on their honeymoon they came to North Carolina to say thank you to Operation Christmas Child.

"You can't make this stuff up," Graham said.

He also told of a girl in Mongolia who received a shoebox and became eager to learn English. She taught herself the language and eventually received a scholarship to Liberty University in Lynchburg, Virginia.

Graham encouraged everyone who packs a shoebox to pray for the child who will receive it, even without knowing where it will go. "God knows that. God knows," he said. "Let's say this year we collect 12 million boxes. Can you imagine 12 million people praying for children?"

The goal, he said, is not simply to hand a child a present, but to share the meaning of Christmas. "We want every child to know the true meaning of Christmas," he said. "I want every child of the world to know that God loves them and that He sent His Son from heaven to this earth. God gave His Son, and that's what Christmas is all about — it's about God giving."

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

A shoebox is a small gift, but in God's hands it can open a door to a family, a church, and a new life in Christ. As the Lord said, "It is more blessed to give than to receive" (Acts 20:35). When we give in Jesus' name and pray for the child who receives it, God knows exactly where that gift is going.
