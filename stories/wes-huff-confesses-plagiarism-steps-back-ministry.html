Apologist Wes Huff Confesses to Plagiarism, Steps Back From Ministry

By GLM Omnimedia News November 22, 2026 Source: Protestia

Christian apologist Wes Huff has publicly confessed to plagiarism, saying he took the work of others and passed it off as his own, and he has stepped back from his ministry and church work while the matter is reviewed.

In a statement released this week, Huff wrote plainly: “I have plagiarized the work of others. This was wrong, and it was sinful.” He said that over the years he had developed justifications for what he did, minimizing how serious it was and at times pushing it so far from his mind that he failed to reckon with what he had done. Those patterns, he said, affected far more of his work than he ever wanted to acknowledge.

The confession follows plagiarism accusations raised against him. Huff said he deeply regrets his first response to those accusations, describing it as defensive and dishonest, and he said he regrets involving others in that response. “I want to face all of this truthfully, accept the consequences of my actions, and do what I can to make things right,” he wrote.

Huff said his heart has always been to tell people about Jesus and to help others discover the beauty and richness of the Bible and its history, but he added that a good desire does not excuse the ways he has fallen short. “I have clearly failed to uphold the standards of integrity that I have encouraged others to value,” he wrote. “That failure is mine, and I take responsibility for it.”

He also spoke of the damage done to the message he has spent his life proclaiming. “Through my own actions, I have undeniably brought reproach upon the very gospel I have spent so much of my life seeking to proclaim,” he wrote. “The truth of the gospel is never served by dishonesty in the way we communicate it.”

To those who trusted him and his work, and to those whose work he treated as his own, Huff offered a direct apology and asked for forgiveness, while acknowledging that forgiveness cannot be demanded and that rebuilding trust, if it is possible, will take time.

As he steps back from ministry and church work, Huff said he is submitting himself fully to an in-depth review by Apologetics Canada and to any academic disciplinary process he will be subject to. He said he will also be under the care and counsel of his church and elders. “Above all, I want to walk through this with genuine repentance before God, and with humility toward those I have wronged,” he wrote, adding that repentance is more than words and must be demonstrated through his actions going forward. He closed by asking for prayer for himself and his family.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

Truthfulness is not optional for those who teach the Word of God; it is the foundation of the whole calling. “The integrity of the upright shall guide them: but the perverseness of transgressors shall destroy them” (Proverbs 11:3). Yet the same Word that exposes sin also opens the door to mercy: “The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit: a broken and a contrite heart, O God, thou wilt not despise” (Psalm 51:17). Confession is the first step of repentance; the fruit of it is proven in what follows.
