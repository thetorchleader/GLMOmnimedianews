The Fall of Jensen Huang: Nvidia’s King Admits Adultery With a Woman U.S. Officials Suspect Is a Chinese Spy — and Proves Why AI Must Be Led by Moral Men

By GLM Omnimedia News November 17, 2026 Source: BCNN1

Jensen Huang built Nvidia from nothing into the most valuable company on earth — the chipmaker whose products power nearly every major artificial intelligence system in the world. This week, that reign collapsed in disgrace.

On Sunday, November 8, Huang admitted in a note to his own employees that he had committed adultery. “I was unfaithful to my wife,” he wrote. “My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did.” According to CNN and The New York Times, the woman is Alice Huang (no relation), an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based Nvidia customer. U.S. officials have told CNN that American intelligence concluded in a classified memo issued in May that Alice Huang is a Chinese intelligence asset, and Megaspeed was already under federal investigation for allegedly diverting highly coveted U.S. semiconductors to entities in China in violation of U.S. export controls.

The spy allegation itself remains unproven in public. Attorney General Todd Blanche said Thursday that the Justice Department has “not yet decided” whether Alice Huang was a spy, though the FBI is investigating. China has rejected the accusation as baseless. Nvidia’s board, which has launched an independent investigation, says it has “no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised.”

What followed the admission only deepened the crisis. The New York Times reported a second extramarital relationship, this one with an Nvidia employee who reports directly to Huang. A shareholder lawsuit filed in California alleges that Nvidia and Huang deceived investors, after the company had publicly described his interactions with Alice Huang as “not unusual or unexpected.” Nvidia’s stock fell as much as 9% in a single day. Analyst Dan Ives estimated that Nvidia is worth roughly $1 trillion less without Huang at the helm, calling his fall “a gut punch for Nvidia and the US tech industry.” And on Thursday, Huang’s exit as chief executive was announced — a founder with no groomed successor, gone.

Daniel Whyte III, president of Gospel Light Society International, said: “This is exactly what I wrote about in my book, The alGODrithm Charter and Framework: The 10 Commandments of Artificial Intelligence. AI must be ruled by people who have morals and morality, because their immorality can color and ruin a good thing. Just as we want moral ministers, moral presidents, and moral congressmen, we must have moral leadership in AI. The Bible says, ‘Be sure your sin will find you out’ (Numbers 32:23), and ‘Righteousness exalteth a nation: but sin is a reproach to any people’ (Proverbs 14:34). One man’s private sin has now shaken a $4.5 trillion company, rattled Wall Street, and raised real questions about the security of the most important technology of our age. Character is not a private matter when God has placed you over something this powerful.”

The damage reaches far beyond one company. Nvidia’s chips are the backbone of the AI race between America and China, and export controls are America’s chief means of keeping the most advanced of those chips out of Beijing’s hands. If the head of that company could be drawn into a relationship with a woman his own government’s intelligence services suspect of spying — while her firm stood accused of smuggling the very chips at issue — then the nation’s whole system of guarding this technology has been shown to have a hole in it no law can patch: the heart of the man at the top.

That is why this story is not merely a business scandal or a celebrity disgrace. It is a warning. Artificial intelligence is a good and powerful gift of human ingenuity, but like every powerful thing, it will take on the character of the people who lead it. America does not only need smarter men running AI. It needs moral men — men who fear God, keep their vows, and cannot be bought, flattered, or trapped.

Let us pray for Jensen Huang’s wife and children, who did nothing to deserve this pain; pray that the investigations now underway uncover the whole truth, whatever it is; and pray that God raises up leaders in this new field who understand that the first commandment of artificial intelligence is the oldest one of all: thou shalt have no other gods before God — not money, not power, and not lust.
