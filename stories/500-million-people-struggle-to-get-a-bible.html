500 Million People Still Struggle to Get a Bible — and Technology Is Opening New Doors

By GLM Omnimedia News November 17, 2026 Source: Charisma News

More than 500 million people around the world still struggle to get a Bible, even as new technology opens doors for Scripture to reach places long closed to the Gospel, CBN News reported.

The barriers go beyond governments that are hostile to Christianity. Poverty, illiteracy, and language differences also keep people from God's Word — including Christians who want a Bible but cannot obtain one.

Ken Bitgood, founder and CEO of the Digital Bible Society, spoke about those challenges with CBN News host George Thomas. The Bible Access Initiative, a research project that began in 2020, examines Bible availability across 106 countries.

The research draws a careful distinction: it looks both at places where obtaining Scripture is most difficult and at countries where the greatest numbers of Christians want a Bible but lack one. As Bitgood put it, the challenges affect “not just persecuted countries, but countries also impacted by poverty or illiteracy or logistical challenges.”

That distinction matters. A Bible shortage can persist where Christianity is legal, because people cannot afford Scripture, cannot read it, or cannot find it in their own language. Bitgood pointed to the impact of poverty in the Democratic Republic of Congo, Ethiopia, and Nigeria, and said Tanzania ranks sixth on the initiative's worldwide shortage list.

In restricted countries, missionaries and ministries are using digital tools to carry Scripture across barriers. Bitgood identified microSD cards as a useful delivery method in Iran and Afghanistan, and audio Scripture and solar-powered players also help people hear God's Word. “It's easier than ever before to bring scriptures digitally into closed access countries,” he told CBN News.

But delivering a Bible also means understanding the needs of the person receiving it. Bitgood pointed to Nigeria's more than 500 languages and dialects, explaining why audio Scripture is essential. “People need the word of God in their own language. They need to be able to hear it,” he said. He described meeting people who had never realized Scripture was available in a language they understood: “Many many times they didn't even know that God's word existed in their own heart language.” He described watching people receive Scripture and “how they devour the Scriptures.”

That hunger should move every believer who owns a Bible. Access to God's Word is a privilege we must never take for granted, while millions are still waiting for the opportunity we have every day. The work of translation, printing, and distribution goes on — and the Church can pray, give, and send so that no one who longs for the Scriptures is left without them.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

The Bible declares, “So then faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God” (Romans 10:17). If faith comes by hearing the Word, then putting a Bible — in print or in audio, in a person's own heart language — into every seeking hand is not a side project of the Church; it is the Great Commission itself. Pray for the translators, the distributors, and the believers who are still waiting for their first copy of God's Word.
