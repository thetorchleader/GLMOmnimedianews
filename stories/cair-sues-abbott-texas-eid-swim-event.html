Texas Woman Sues Governor Abbott Over Canceled Eid Swim Event

By GLM Omnimedia News November 18, 2026 Source: Religion News Service

A Dallas woman and the Council on American-Islamic Relations have filed a federal lawsuit against Texas Gov. Greg Abbott, the city of Grand Prairie, and the Epic Waters Indoor Waterpark, saying pressure from the governor led to the cancellation of a private Eid swim celebration last May.

The lawsuit, filed Tuesday, November 17, in the U.S. District Court for the Western Division of Texas, alleges that Abbott abused his executive power by threatening to withhold $530,000 in state funding from the city unless the event was canceled. The governor's office did not respond to a request for comment, and a spokesperson for Grand Prairie said the city had no statement at this time. No court has yet ruled on the claims, and the allegations in the lawsuit have not been proven.

Aminah Knight, a Dallas educator and daycare owner, organized the swim event, called "DFW Epic Eid," at the waterpark for two years. The lawsuit says the 2025 gathering drew 600 people and was meant as a family-friendly way to celebrate Eid, a Muslim religious holiday.

According to the filing, Knight entered into a contract with the waterpark for the 2026 event and promoted it online and with flyers that described it as "Muslim only." Knight said the waterpark approved the flyer and that she had a good working relationship with its staff.

In early May, Knight apologized and updated her website to make clear that the event was open to anyone, asking only that attendees observe a modest dress code, the filing says.

On May 6, Governor Abbott said publicly that the city of Grand Prairie "must cancel the event and commit to never allowing something like it again," calling the Muslim swim day religious discrimination. Later that same day, a waterpark supervisor called Knight to tell her the event had been canceled. In an email on May 7, Epic Waters General Manager Jessi O'Daniel wrote that the cancellation was because of a missing deposit, according to the lawsuit.

Knight disputes that account. The lawsuit says she followed all of the contract's requirements and that waterpark staff did not answer her repeated emails asking whether the decision was connected to the governor's pressure. "The waterpark loved having us there, but as soon as the controversy hit, they said nothing," Knight said. "To date, they said nothing, and that's really upsetting, and I feel like they need to step up."

Knight told Religion News Service that she faced harassment and threats after the governor criticized the event, and that she took the past few months to heal and reflect before deciding to seek justice through the courts. She called the city's decision "a classic case of peer pressure."

The lawsuit asks the court to rule that canceling the 2026 event violated Knight's First Amendment rights and to issue an injunction allowing her to host the swim party in 2027. She is also seeking compensation for lost profits and the fees she paid refunding tickets, along with attorney's fees.

The case comes one day after Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton withdrew a lawsuit that had sought to ban CAIR and its Texas chapters from operating in the state. Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa has also said she will remove the state's "foreign terrorist organization" label against CAIR. The group welcomed that pledge, saying in a statement that "anti-Muslim hate is no longer an effective political weapon in Texas, and it must no longer have any place in state governance."

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

Whatever the court decides, the principle is plain: government should not punish people for gathering to celebrate their faith. Scripture calls rulers to judge righteously and show no partiality (Deuteronomy 1:17), and Christians are told to pray for those in authority, "that we may lead a quiet and peaceable life in all godliness and honesty" (1 Timothy 2:2). Religious freedom that only protects the majority is no freedom at all — it must cover every neighbor's right to worship and gather in peace.
