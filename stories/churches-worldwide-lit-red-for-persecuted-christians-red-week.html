Churches Around the World Glow Red to Remember Persecuted Christians

By GLM Omnimedia News November 18, 2026 Source: Christian Post

Churches, cathedrals, monuments, and public buildings around the world are glowing red this week as Christians mark Red Week, a yearly witness to the suffering of believers who are persecuted for their faith. The display, organized by the charity Aid to the Church in Need, has been held every year since 2015, and this year at least 130 cathedrals and many other churches are taking part.

The red light stands for the blood of the martyrs. In a video message, Aid to the Church in Need's international executive president, Regina Lynch, said the churches are lit red so that suffering Christians will know they are not forgotten. "We are illuminating churches and other buildings in red, the red of the blood of the martyrs. We wear red, but most importantly, we are praying for these Christians," she said. "They often feel alone. They feel isolated, and they need hope, and this is the hope we can give them through our prayers."

Red Week is more than a display of light. Prayer vigils, testimonies, public events, and school programs are running through the month of November, and the charity expects about 125,000 people to take part worldwide. The European Parliament building in Strasbourg, France, is scheduled to be lit red on November 23.

France has become the center of the observance in Europe, with the historic Chartres Cathedral among the landmarks bathed in red light. About 80 churches and other sites across France are participating, and some 4,000 volunteers are carrying the commemoration into rural communities. In the United Kingdom, believers are marking Red Wednesday with a Mass at St. George's Cathedral in Southwark, London, while hundreds of young people are expected to gather in Birmingham. In Spain, more than 250 churches, cathedrals, monuments, and public buildings are lit red, and local communities are leading the observance in countries such as Croatia and Romania.

The witness comes at a grim moment. In a report released last month, Aid to the Church in Need found that persecution has not improved in any of the 18 countries it monitors over the past two years, and has grown worse in more than half of them. The report pointed to extremist violence, authoritarian governments, and religious nationalism pressing harder on Christian communities, while conflict is driving many believers from their homes in parts of Africa and Asia. Christians in the Middle East, the report warned, fear for the survival of their faith in its ancient birthplace amid continued political upheaval and war.

Open Doors U.S. reached a similar conclusion in its annual World Watch List published earlier this year, estimating that about 388 million Christians — more than one in seven believers worldwide — faced intense persecution or discrimination for their faith in 2025, an increase of more than 8 million over the previous year's report.

For many of those believers, loneliness is part of the suffering. In Somalia, for example, there are only hundreds of Christians in a Muslim-majority country of more than 19 million people, and many must worship in secret. Open Doors has urged prayer that such believers would know they are not alone, even when no one around them shares their faith.

Aid to the Church in Need is asking Christians everywhere to take part in whatever way they can: attend a local event, wear red, and above all, pray for those who suffer for the name of Jesus Christ.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

The red light is a reminder that the suffering Church is still part of our own body. "Remember them that are in bonds, as bound with them; and them which suffer adversity, as being yourselves also in the body" (Hebrews 13:3). When we pray for persecuted believers, we are not praying for strangers — we are remembering our own brothers and sisters, and the Lord who said, "Blessed are they which are persecuted for righteousness' sake: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven" (Matthew 5:10).
