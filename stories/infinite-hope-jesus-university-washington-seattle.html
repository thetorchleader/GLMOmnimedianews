The Hope of Jesus Is Reaching Students at the University of Washington

By GLM Omnimedia News November 17, 2026 Source: CBN News

Students at the University of Washington in Seattle recently gathered in the heart of campus for a student-led worship event, spending hours together in prayer and praise. The gathering took place in Red Square, the university's central plaza, and it drew young people who are hungry for something more than lectures and grades.

Seattle has been named the least religious city in America for two years running in a Pew Research Center poll. Yet on that campus, the light of Jesus is breaking through, CBN News reported.

Pastor Jesse Bradley shared the Gospel with the crowd, gave away Bibles and Try Praying booklets, and prayed with any student who was open to it. Bradley is a former professional soccer player who now pastors Grace Community Church in the Seattle area and serves as a chaplain for the Seattle Sounders and the U.S. Men's National Team.

His goal, he told CBN News, was to share what he calls the infinite hope of Jesus with the students gathered there. He described a hope that is not a dead religion but a living relationship with God — forgiveness by grace, freedom from guilt and shame, and a Good Shepherd who leads His people.

The Red Square gathering is part of a larger work on campus. Bradley's church has been hosting gatherings this year called MidweekHope.org in Kane Hall, where students study Scripture, experience authentic community, and share testimonies. Bradley reports that hundreds of Bibles have been distributed, and he says students are finding the meetings inspiring and refreshing.

“Students are hungry spiritually; life is more than just academics,” Bradley said, pointing to the question Jesus asked: what does it profit a person to gain the whole world yet forfeit their soul?

Bradley knows that hunger from the inside. He says he was an atheist during his freshman year at Dartmouth College, until a religion class led him to read the Gospel of John. The words of Jesus caught his attention, then his devotion, and he came to believe that Christ gives abundant and eternal life and a new identity that nothing can separate from the love of God.

His ministry reaches beyond the campus as well. In September, Bradley helped lead a Faith and Family Celebration at a Sounders game at Lumen Field, where former MLS All-Star Paul Arriola shared how his relationship with God helped him through family tragedies and injuries. Bradley and other evangelists also reached out to fans during the U.S.-hosted World Cup over the summer, pointing people to victory through Jesus.

The work in Seattle is a reminder that no city is too far gone for the Gospel. Wherever students gather and the Word is opened, the same Jesus who met a young atheist at Dartmouth is still meeting young people today — and the hope He gives is available to everyone.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

The Bible says, “Now the God of hope fill you with all joy and peace in believing, that ye may abound in hope, through the power of the Holy Ghost” (Romans 15:13). Hope is not a feeling we manufacture; it is a Person we meet. Wherever the Gospel is faithfully shared — even in the least religious city in America — the God of hope is still filling hearts, and the Church should keep sowing the Word and praying for the harvest.
