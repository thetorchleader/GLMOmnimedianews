Kevin DeYoung: Suicide Is a Sin, but It Does Not Decide a Christian's Eternity

By GLM Omnimedia News November 21, 2026 Source: ChurchLeaders

Pastor, author, and theologian Kevin DeYoung recently took up one of the hardest questions a Christian can ask: if a believer takes his or her own life, does that act decide where that person spends eternity?

DeYoung, senior pastor of Christ Covenant Church in Matthews, North Carolina, addressed the question during a live Clearly Reformed question-and-answer event, according to ChurchLeaders. His answer was careful on both sides: Scripture plainly calls suicide a sin, yet the Bible does not teach that a single act — even a final one — undoes the salvation of a true believer.

"We know that the sixth commandment forbids murder," DeYoung said. "It is a sin for someone to harm themselves. It is a sin for someone to end their own life. It's a violation of the sixth commandment." The commandment he cited is plain: "Thou shalt not kill" (Exodus 20:13).

DeYoung noted that the Bible records several deaths by suicide, and none of them is held up as noble or worthy of imitation. He pointed to Judas Iscariot as an example. In Scripture, suicide is never presented as a way to preserve dignity or to do something honorable. It is always a tragedy.

But when he turned to the question of whether suicide settles a person's eternal destiny, DeYoung said, "I think the short answer is no." A Christian can pass through seasons of deep darkness, depression, and doubt without losing salvation, he explained. "Lots of Christians have had melancholy in deep depression," he said, describing believers who struggle at times to sense the presence of God.

DeYoung's point, as reported by ChurchLeaders, is that while suicide is a tragic and final sinful act, it does not by itself prove that the person was never genuinely saved. Salvation rests on what Christ has done, not on the manner of a person's death or on dying in a moment of spiritual strength.

That is a word of comfort for families who have buried a loved one after suicide and have wondered ever since whether that loved one is with the Lord. The Bible's answer is that no sin a believer commits — past, present, or final — is greater than the blood of Jesus Christ. "The blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin" (1 John 1:7).

At the same time, DeYoung's answer is no excuse for treating life lightly. Life belongs to God, who gives it and who alone has the right to take it. The Christian who is suffering must be pointed toward help, toward prayer, toward the church, and toward the hope that God is near to the brokenhearted. "The LORD is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit" (Psalm 34:18).

If you or someone you love is thinking about suicide, please reach out right away. In the United States, you can call or text 988 at any hour to reach the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline for free, confidential help. Tell a pastor, a family member, or a trusted friend today. Your life matters to God, and He has not finished His work in you.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

No act of ours — not even our last one — can undo what Christ has finished. "For I am persuaded, that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come, nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature, shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord" (Romans 8:38-39). If you are in darkness, reach out for help today: God is near to the brokenhearted, and your life is precious to Him.
