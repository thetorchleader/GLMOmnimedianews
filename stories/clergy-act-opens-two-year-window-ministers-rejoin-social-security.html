New Law Opens Two-Year Window for Ministers To Rejoin Social Security

By GLM Omnimedia News November 15, 2026 Source: ChurchLeaders

A new federal law gives ministers who once opted out of Social Security a rare second chance to opt back in. The U.S. Senate unanimously passed the Clergy Act on September 30, following passage in the House earlier this year, and President Trump later signed it into law, according to a report from ChurchLeaders.

The law creates a temporary, two-year window for eligible clergy to re-enter Social Security during tax years 2029 and 2030. For ministers who made a decision decades ago that they believed could never be undone, the window is a meaningful opportunity to reconsider.

Under the law as it has long stood, a qualifying minister may opt out of Social Security coverage for ministerial earnings on the basis of religious opposition to accepting certain public insurance benefits. But that decision is generally irrevocable. A minister who later changes his or her mind cannot simply elect to participate again.

The Clergy Act provides a temporary exception to that rule. Clergy who use the new opt-in window will begin paying Social Security taxes on their ministerial earnings. Consistent with current eligibility requirements, they generally will need to earn 40 Social Security credits — typically requiring about 10 years of covered work — to qualify for Social Security retirement benefits.

Congress has opened similar windows for clergy before, including in 1977, 1986, and 1999. Each time, the idea was the same: give ministers who opted out in their younger years a fair chance to reconsider as their circumstances and convictions change.

The law also directs the Internal Revenue Service, in consultation with the Social Security Administration, to develop a plan for informing eligible clergy about the opportunity. Within 90 days after enactment, the IRS must submit that plan to the House Ways and Means Committee and the Senate Finance Committee. The IRS will also develop the forms clergy will use to revoke their previous exemptions.

The Clergy Act was the result of several years of work by the Church Alliance, which advocates on benefits issues for 35 denominational benefit organizations representing a broad range of Judeo-Christian faith traditions. The bipartisan legislation was led by Reps. Vince Fong (R-Calif.) and Mike Thompson (D-Calif.) in the House and Sens. Katie Britt (R-Ala.) and Maggie Hassan (D-N.H.) in the Senate.

For ministers who previously opted out of Social Security, the key dates to remember are 2029 and 2030. Eligible ministers will need to make the election during one of those two tax years, and the deadline is expected to run through the due date for the 2030 federal income tax return, including extensions.

The IRS and the Social Security Administration are expected to provide additional guidance explaining how eligible ministers can opt back in. Until then, pastors and their families would do well to begin the conversation now — with their spouses, their church boards, and trusted financial counsel — so that when the window opens, the decision is made with prayer and a clear mind rather than in haste.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

The Bible says, &ldquo;But if any provide not for his own, and specially for those of his own house, he hath denied the faith, and is worse than an infidel&rdquo; (1 Timothy 5:8). A pastor's calling is to care for the flock God has given him, and that care includes wise provision for his own household &mdash; in the years of ministry and in the years that follow. This new window is worth prayerful consideration by every minister it touches.
