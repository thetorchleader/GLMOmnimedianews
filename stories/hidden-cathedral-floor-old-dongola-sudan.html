Hidden Cathedral Floor Found in Sudan Reveals a Monumental Medieval Church

By GLM Omnimedia News November 17, 2026 Source: CBN News

Archaeologists working in the sands of northern Sudan have reached the original floor of a monumental medieval church, revealing the scale of what may be the largest Christian building ever found in Nubia.

The discovery took place in Old Dongola, the ancient capital of the Christian Kingdom of Makuria. A Polish-Sudanese team uncovered a staircase with walls 11 metres (36 feet) high that led down to the floor level of the Great Church of Jesus, a building first detected in 2021 but still mostly buried beneath the sand.

Artur Obłuski, who leads the excavation and directs the Polish Center of Mediterranean Archaeology, said the staircase is the only place where the team has so far been able to reach the floor. He described the building as the largest and most important church of medieval Makuria, one of three Christian kingdoms that grew up along the middle Nile after missionaries brought the Gospel to the region in the sixth century.

The researchers estimate the church measured about 47 metres (154 feet) wide and 57 metres (187 feet) long — a footprint the mission says is larger than the White House in Washington. Obłuski compared its importance to that of Hagia Sophia in Istanbul, saying the Great Church of Jesus may have set the pattern for church building across Nubia and northeastern Africa, just as Hagia Sophia did for the Byzantine world.

Old Dongola stands on the eastern bank of the Nile, about 160 kilometres (100 miles) southeast of the modern city of Dongola, and is known for its many ancient churches. The team plans to keep excavating the great church in the seasons ahead, hoping to establish its full size and its place in the life of the kingdom.

Some caution is in order. Sudan’s antiquities ministry has noted that the latest findings come from the 2025 field season rather than new digging this month, and that identifying the building as the city’s cathedral remains a working hypothesis that needs more research. The traditional view has long placed Old Dongola’s cathedral outside the city walls, while this great church stands inside them.

Even with those questions still open, the find is a powerful reminder of a chapter of Christian history many believers have never heard: for centuries, a thriving Christian kingdom worshipped, built, and buried its dead along the Nile in what is now Sudan, long before the region’s churches faded from the world’s memory.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

The Church Jesus promised to build has outlasted kingdoms, empires, and centuries of sand. “Upon this rock I will build my church; and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it” (Matthew 16:18). What was buried in Sudan for a thousand years still testifies that the Gospel once took deep root in Africa — and that no place or people is beyond the reach of Christ’s Church.
