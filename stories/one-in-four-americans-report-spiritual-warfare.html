One in Four Americans Say They Have Faced Spiritual Warfare, Survey Finds

By GLM Omnimedia News November 7, 2026 Source: CBN News

A new survey from the American Bible Society finds that about one in four American adults — 26 percent — say they have experienced spiritual warfare, a reality the Bible describes plainly in Ephesians 6. The findings come from the organization's State of the Bible series, which asked Americans whether they believe they have faced a spiritual attack.

The experience is far more common among people who practice their faith regularly. According to Dr. John Plake, editor-in-chief of the State of the Bible series and chief innovation officer at the American Bible Society, 58 percent of practicing Christians said they have faced spiritual warfare. The figure drops to 28 percent among casual Christians, 24 percent among nominal Christians, and 10 percent among non-Christians.

Plake told CBN News that the survey measures what people believe they have experienced, and he acknowledged that a spiritual attack can be difficult to define. The survey did not collect detailed accounts of what respondents believed an attack looked like or involved, and there is limited deep data on the subject. Still, he said he was struck by how high the number was, and he believes many of those surveyed could describe times when the troubles they faced seemed to go beyond natural causes.

The survey also asked people how they respond when they believe they are under spiritual attack. Prayer was by far the most common answer: 74 percent of those who said they had faced an attack said they pray in response. Another 36 percent said they declare the authority of Christ, and 24 percent said they listen to praise and worship music.

The smallest number, and the one Plake found most concerning, was the 15 percent who said they meditate on or quote Scripture when they sense spiritual opposition. He said he would have hoped that figure would be higher, and he sees it as a call for pastors and church leaders to better equip believers to answer spiritual attacks with the Word of God.

The findings point to a gap worth honest reflection. Many Christians recognize they are in a spiritual battle and turn to prayer — and prayer is right and necessary. But the Bible itself gives believers more than prayer alone: it gives them the sword of the Spirit, which is the Word of God, and the full armor described in Ephesians 6. A believer who prays but does not know the Scriptures is fighting with one hand tied.

Churches have a clear opportunity here. Teaching believers to memorize and speak Scripture, to declare the finished work of Christ, and to stand firm in the truth is not an extra for the spiritually advanced — it is basic equipping for every Christian, because the battle, as Scripture says, is not against flesh and blood.

For believers who have sensed spiritual opposition in their own lives, the survey's message is encouraging: they are not alone, and the tools God has given are sufficient. Prayer, the authority of Christ, worship, and the Word together form the pattern of a believer who stands — and keeps standing.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

The battle is real, and the Bible says so plainly: "For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places" (Ephesians 6:12, KJV). God has not left His people unarmed — He has given prayer, the authority of Christ, worship, and the sword of the Spirit, which is the Word of God. Stand in all of them.
