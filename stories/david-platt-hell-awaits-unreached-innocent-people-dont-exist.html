David Platt: No One Is Innocent — Why the Unreached Still Need the Gospel

By GLM Omnimedia News November 25, 2026 Source: ChurchLeaders

What happens to people who live and die without ever hearing the Gospel of Jesus Christ? Pastor David Platt says the answer from Scripture is sobering: they do not go to heaven simply because they never heard — because, in his words, “innocent people don’t exist.”

Platt made the case in a message titled “Reach the Unreached,” delivered to The Porch, the young-adult gathering at Watermark Community Church in Dallas, on September 13, 2022. ChurchLeaders revisited the message this week, and Platt’s argument has not changed since he first preached it from Southern Baptist Convention pulpits as early as 2011.

At the time of the Dallas message, Platt was pastor of McLean Bible Church near Washington, D.C. He is the founder of the global ministry Radical, the author of “Radical: Taking Back Your Faith From the American Dream,” and served as president of the Southern Baptist Convention’s International Mission Board from 2014 to 2018.

Platt’s answer begins with a careful distinction. He says God does not hold people accountable for a Gospel they never heard. He does hold them accountable for rejecting the God who has already shown himself to them in creation. That guilt, he says, is exactly why they need the Gospel.

“They’re not accountable for what they’ve not heard,” Platt told the Dallas crowd, “but that doesn’t mean they go to heaven. They’re condemned for rejecting the God that has been clearly shown to them.”

He then argued that the common hope — that God’s love will simply let unreached people into heaven — defeats itself. If never hearing the Gospel guaranteed heaven, then taking the Gospel to unreached people would be the worst thing a Christian could do for them, because the moment they heard it they would face the risk of hell for the first time. “If they have not heard the Gospel and that is some kind of pass into heaven,” Platt said, then bringing the good news would mean “now there’s a chance [they] can go to hell.” As he put it, they would say, “Just keep it to yourself!”

Platt builds his case on Romans 1 through 3, which he reads as a single argument from the Apostle Paul, and he lays it out in four truths. First, all people know God: his eternal power and divine nature are plain in what he made, so no one has an excuse (Romans 1:18-20). Second, all people reject God: they knew him but refused to honor him or thank him, and traded him for idols (Romans 1:21-23). Third, all people are guilty: no one is righteous and no one truly seeks God (Romans 3:10-12). Fourth, all people stand condemned for rejecting God, whether or not they have heard of Jesus (Romans 3:19-20).

“Regardless of whether or not someone has heard the Gospel, all people stand guilty before God and sin and deserve eternal separation from him,” Platt said. But the good news follows at once: Paul writes that God now gives his righteousness through faith in Jesus Christ to everyone who believes (Romans 3:21-22).

Platt also explained what the word “unreached” actually means. An unreached people group is an ethnic group without enough followers of Jesus to evangelize its own people. The Joshua Project, the most widely used database, sets the line at less than 2 percent evangelical Christians or less than 5 percent Christian adherents. A person born into such a group, Platt said, would likely live and die without ever hearing the Gospel.

“What that word ‘unreached’ means is not just lost,” he told the crowd. “Lost people live in every city in America. Unreached people live where there is no church, no Christian and no Bible within reach.” At the 2011 SBC annual meeting he put it this way: “Unreached means that you don’t even have access to hear the gospel.”

Platt’s message ended where it began: with a call. He urged his listeners not to sit back and “waste your life” chasing “a comfortable Christian spin on the American dream” while so many people remain unreached. The question he leaves with the Church is not whether the unreached are guilty — Scripture, he says, has settled that — but whether those who have heard will go and tell them.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

<p>The Bible leaves no room for the hope that ignorance is innocence. “For all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God” (Romans 3:23), and “there is none righteous, no, not one” (Romans 3:10). Yet the same passage that condemns us also saves us: “the righteousness of God which is by faith of Jesus Christ unto all and upon all them that believe” (Romans 3:22). And that is why the Gospel must go: “How shall they hear without a preacher?” (Romans 10:14). If we have heard, we have been given the message — and the unreached are waiting on us to carry it.</p>
