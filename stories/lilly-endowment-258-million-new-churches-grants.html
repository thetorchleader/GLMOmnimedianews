Lilly Endowment Gives $258 Million to Help Start New Churches Across America

By GLM Omnimedia News November 20, 2026 Source: Religion News Service

INDIANAPOLIS — Lilly Endowment Inc. has approved 52 grants totaling more than $258 million to help churches across America start new congregations, the foundation announced on Friday.

The grants, made through the Endowment’s New Churches Initiative, range from $4 million to $5 million each and will run for five years. The money will go to denominational organizations, church networks, and other faith-based organizations working to establish and support new churches.

The organizations receiving grants include many of the major Christian denominations in the United States, along with non-denominational church-planting organizations. Together they represent mainline Protestant, Catholic, Orthodox, and evangelical traditions, as well as Asian American, Hispanic, and Black church communities.

The Endowment launched the invitational New Churches Initiative after conversations with religious leaders who reported that new congregations tend to be more innovative and adaptable than established ones. According to the Endowment, newer churches are especially good at reaching people who are not currently connected to a church or parish, and they generally draw more young people, are often led by younger pastors, and are more likely to serve growing immigrant populations.

“Leaders from many Christian denominations, parachurch organizations and church networks shared encouraging accounts about the growth and vitality of new congregations they are starting,” said Christopher L. Coble, the Endowment’s vice president for religion. “We hope these grants will help to build their capacities to strengthen and extend these important efforts.”

The organizations will use their five-year grants in three ways. First, they will design and test new strategies and practices for establishing new congregations and parishes. Second, they will strengthen their own organizational capacities to support those congregations. And third, they will build up pools of funds to provide direct financial support to new churches as they work to become self-sustaining.

The grant activities will include strengthening the recruitment of pastoral leaders for new churches through vocational discernment retreats, sharing the stories of pastors who lead new congregations, and hosting conferences for pastors and seminarians who are considering pastoring new churches. They will also expand training and support for the pastors of new churches through new digital platforms, mentoring, and events focused on coaching and spiritual care.

Other work will include establishing new congregations with lay-centered leadership structures and lay-led worship services, starting congregations embedded within other community-serving organizations, creating new structures and teams to strengthen entrepreneurial church planting, improving fundraising and communications strategies, and strengthening the way organizations gather and use data about their ministries.

Lilly Endowment Inc. is an Indianapolis-based private philanthropic foundation created in 1937 by J.K. Lilly Sr. and his sons Eli and J.K. Jr. through gifts of stock in their pharmaceutical business, Eli Lilly and Company. The Endowment is a separate entity from the company, with its own governing board, staff, and location. A principal aim of its religion grantmaking is to deepen and enrich the lives of Christians in the United States by supporting efforts that strengthen the vitality of congregations and the pastoral and lay leadership of Christian communities.

The complete list of grants can be found on the Endowment’s website. The New Churches Initiative complements the Endowment’s other efforts to help established Christian congregations adapt to their changing contexts and enhance the vitality of their ministries.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

Money can open doors, but only the Lord can build His church: “Except the LORD build the house, they labour in vain that build it” (Psalm 127:1). This is a hopeful investment in new congregations, and it is worth praying that every dollar serves the Great Commission — “Go ye therefore, and teach all nations” (Matthew 28:19) — and that these new churches preach Christ crucified and make disciples who last.
