New Rankings Show Where Religious Freedom Thrives — and Where It Struggles — Across America

By GLM Omnimedia News November 19, 2026 Source: Charisma News

Where you live in America can make a real difference in how freely a church, a Christian school, or a ministry can carry out its calling. That is the finding of a newly released national report, the Napa Legal Institute's 2026 Faith and Freedom Index, which examined the laws affecting churches, Christian schools, pregnancy resource centers, and other religious nonprofits in all 50 states and Washington, D.C.

According to Charisma News, reporting on Fox News Digital's coverage of the index, researchers evaluated 15 areas of state law, measuring both the religious liberty protections on the books and the regulatory burdens placed on faith-based organizations.

The results reveal a wide divide. Alabama earned the highest score in the nation at 80 percent, followed by Kansas at 76 percent, Indiana at 75 percent, and Texas at 71 percent. At the other end of the rankings, Michigan scored lowest at 34 percent, with Washington at 38 percent and Massachusetts at 41 percent. Florida received an overall score of 67 percent.

The index also found that a state's political leaning does not by itself determine how well it protects religious freedom. New York, a heavily Democratic state, outperformed Republican-led Tennessee, North Dakota, and South Carolina in the rankings. Connecticut and Illinois have religious freedom restoration acts on the books, while Alaska and Ohio do not, the institute noted.

Frank DeVito, senior counsel at Napa Legal, told Fox News Digital that Michigan's low ranking reflects what he described as burdensome nondiscrimination requirements, insufficient religious exemptions, and regulatory obstacles facing faith-based organizations in the state.

For Christian schools, those kinds of laws can create direct conflicts over hiring practices and admissions policies rooted in religious conviction. For ministries serving struggling families and vulnerable communities, costly reporting requirements and the threat of legal battles can drain resources meant for outreach and the preaching of the Gospel.

That is the concern DeVito raised in speaking with Fox News Digital. "We need religious organizations to thrive," he said.

As state legislatures continue to shape the laws governing religious organizations, the ability of churches and ministries to operate according to their convictions can depend heavily on where they are located. For believers, the rankings are a reminder that religious liberty is not guaranteed by geography — it must be guarded, prayed for, and defended in every state, whatever its politics.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

Scripture calls believers to stand fast in the liberty Christ has given: "Stand fast therefore in the liberty wherewith Christ hath made us free" (Galatians 5:1). Freedom to worship, teach, and serve according to conscience is a gift from God, not a favor from government — and where it is threatened, the Church is called to pray, to speak truth with grace, and to stand firm.
