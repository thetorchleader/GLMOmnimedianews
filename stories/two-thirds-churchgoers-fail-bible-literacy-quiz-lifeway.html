Two in Three Churchgoers Fail Bible Literacy Quiz, Lifeway Research Finds

By GLM Omnimedia News November 4, 2026 Source: The Christian Post

A new study from Lifeway Research finds that most American churchgoers struggle to answer basic questions about the Bible. According to a report released Tuesday, November 3, the average churchgoer scored just 51 out of 100 on a Bible knowledge quiz, and about two in three earned a failing grade.

The survey, conducted online August 12–29, included 2,012 Americans who identify as Protestant, nondenominational, Catholic, or Orthodox Christian and who attend church in a typical month. The margin of error was plus or minus 2.2 percentage points at the 95 percent confidence level, according to the Christian Post's report on the study.

Respondents answered dozens of questions across eight areas, including the people of the Bible, the order of biblical events, and which books belong in Scripture. Scores were graded on a familiar letter scale: 90 and above earned an A, 80 to 89 a B, 70 to 79 a C, 60 to 69 a D, and below 60 an F.

The results were sobering. Sixty-five percent of respondents earned an F, while 9 percent earned a D, 9 percent a C, 8 percent a B, and 9 percent an A. In other words, fewer than one in five churchgoers scored a B or better.

Some of the missed questions covered material many would consider basic. While 94 percent knew that Moses received the Ten Commandments, only 45 percent correctly named Saul as Israel's first king. About two-thirds wrongly identified Hezekiah — a king of Judah, not a book — as a book of the Bible.

One pattern stood out clearly: the more often people read the Bible, the better they did. Those who read Scripture every day averaged a score of 66, compared with 50 for those who read once a week and 36 for those who rarely or never read it.

Lifeway Research Executive Director Scott McConnell said Bible literacy grows through both reading and studying — working to understand each passage while also moving regularly through the whole Bible so its story stays familiar. Knowing the Bible, he added, involves the heart, the mind, and the emotions, shaped by a desire to know God and to be encouraged by His Word.

Writing about the findings for The Gospel Coalition, author and Bible teacher Jen Wilkin said the results reflect a broader literacy problem in the wider culture. Many Christians, she observed, consume sermons, podcasts, and short devotionals, yet spend little time reading the Bible itself.

The Lifeway findings echo earlier research. A 2019 Pew Research Center study likewise found gaps in religious knowledge, including among self-identified evangelicals — more than a third did not know Jesus was from Nazareth, and about a quarter thought the Golden Rule was one of the Ten Commandments.

For churches, the message of the new study is less about a test score than about a habit. Bible knowledge does not grow by accident; it grows where God's people open the Book, read it steadily, and study it together, week after week.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

As Psalm 119:105 declares, 'Thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path.' A church that reads, studies, and obeys the Bible will not merely know about God; it will know Him.
