Talarico Wins Texas Senate Seat, Invokes David and Jesus in Victory Speech

By GLM Omnimedia News November 5, 2026 Source: Protestia (via Christian Post)

Texas state Rep. James Talarico defeated Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton in the state's U.S. Senate race on Tuesday, and in his victory speech in Austin he reached for the Bible to frame the win -- comparing himself to David facing Goliath and to Jesus cleansing the Temple, according to the Christian Post.

"I'm reminded of that story from Scripture of a shepherd boy who goes up against a giant," Talarico told supporters shortly before midnight. "For 40 days, Goliath teases and taunts. On the day of the fight, he shows up in full armor. He's carrying so many weapons; he needs someone else to carry his shield."

The 37-year-old lawmaker and Presbyterian seminarian, who made Christian language central to his campaign, said his race faced its own giants -- including what he described as a $168 million advertising blitz funded by Elon Musk. "In this race, we went up against our own Goliaths: the richest man in the world, the most powerful man in the world and the entire Washington, D.C. establishment," he said.

"David wasn't as big or as strong as Goliath. He didn't have as many weapons, but he had hope. He had faith, and he had a slingshot," Talarico continued. "You were my slingshot. The people of Texas were my slingshot. And tonight, and tonight, we defeated Goliath."

Talarico, who won with 55 percent of the vote, described his campaign as a "politics of love" that united Texans "from all backgrounds and beliefs onto one team to take on this broken, corrupt political system." His victory was the first statewide Democratic win in Texas since 1994 and the party's first U.S. Senate win in the state since 1988. When he is sworn in next year, he will be the youngest member of the U.S. Senate.

Paxton, the state's three-term attorney general, ran as a staunch ally of President Donald Trump. He was impeached by the Texas House of Representatives in 2023 over allegations of corruption, bribery, abuse of office and obstruction of justice, and was later acquitted by the Texas Senate. Talarico repeatedly called Paxton "the most corrupt politician in America" during the campaign.

Closing his speech, Talarico returned to Scripture, repeating a line he has used throughout his campaign. "Two thousand years ago, when the people at the top hurt His neighbors, that barefoot rabbi didn't stay in a room and pray. He walked into the seat of power, and He flipped over the tables of injustice," he said. "As this movement heads to the seat of power, as we head to the United States Senate, we know what this moment requires of us, Texas. It's time to start flipping tables."

It is good when public figures reach for the Bible -- but the Bible is not a campaign prop. David did not credit his sling or his courage when he faced Goliath; he declared, "the battle is the LORD's," and said God would deliver the giant into his hand "that all the earth may know that there is a God in Israel" (1 Samuel 17:46-47). And when Jesus overturned the tables in the Temple, He was cleansing His Father's house, not launching a political movement (John 2:13-17).

Believers should test every use of Scripture in public life -- including their own -- against the text itself. The Word of God is the standard by which every candidate, every campaign and every heart is measured.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway
