Pastor Declared 'Restored' Eight Months After Admitting to Affair — What Does Biblical Restoration Require?

By GLM Omnimedia News November 22, 2026 Source: Protestia

Eight months after stepping down from ministry over admitted sexual misconduct, pastor Ted Shuttlesworth Jr. has been publicly declared restored to ministry during a conference service — a declaration that has raised serious questions about what true biblical restoration requires.

Shuttlesworth, who co-pastored Miracle Word Church with his wife Carolyn, stepped down in March of this year after admitting he had engaged in "loose and inappropriate conversations" with multiple women and at least one affair, according to Protestia. The outlet also reported that several women contacted them afterward alleging that Shuttlesworth had sent them inappropriate messages, with some of the allegations going back 15 years. Those additional claims are allegations reported by Protestia; Shuttlesworth's own admission concerned the conversations and the affair.

This month, Shuttlesworth and his wife attended the "In Christ" Conference hosted by Mark Hankins Ministries, where Ted has been helping to lead worship. During a Wednesday evening service, Hankins spent nearly ten minutes speaking a prophetic word over the couple, declaring that God was dressing them in "new clothes, clean clothes, and clothes of honor," and that they would carry fresh revelation and preaching power in a new season.

Hankins told the couple they could now declare "I'm ready," saying they had made "the adjustments and the things that had to change." He also warned that "every lie of the enemy shall fall to the ground and everyone that would speak against you, their mouth shall be stopped."

Protestia reports that it is unclear what adjustments have actually been made. According to the outlet, Shuttlesworth has not offered a full public confession or accounting for his actions, and has not contacted the women who came forward to apologize. When Protestia contacted him about a month ago about whether the church would reopen, Shuttlesworth said in a statement that "no such plans have been discussed between my wife and me, nor have any discussions taken place with our board regarding a reopening."

The episode puts a spotlight on a question every church must answer carefully: when a pastor falls into serious sin, what does genuine restoration look like, and who gets to declare it?

Scripture is clear that restoration is real. Galatians 6:1 instructs believers to restore a brother caught in sin "in a spirit of gentleness." The apostle Paul himself was restored after persecuting the church, and Peter was restored after denying Christ. Grace is not in short supply.

But Scripture is equally clear that restoration to ministry is not automatic, instant, or declared by a conference platform. The qualifications for an overseer in 1 Timothy 3 require a man to be "above reproach" and of good reputation. Repentance that restores trust must be visible: confession, accountability, restitution where possible, and the fruit of a changed life proven over time — not merely the passage of a few months and a prophetic pronouncement.

The danger here is not that a fallen pastor would be forgiven and restored. The danger is a restoration pipeline that skips the hard work of repentance and accountability, tells critics to be silent, and puts a man back in the pulpit before the wounded have been cared for and the truth has been fully faced. That is not grace; it is enablement dressed in spiritual language.

Believers should pray for Ted Shuttlesworth Jr. and his family — for genuine repentance, for healing for every woman affected, and for the church to hold fast to both grace and truth. The same Lord who restores the fallen also guards His flock, and He has given His church clear standards for those who would lead it.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

Scripture teaches that a brother caught in sin should be restored "in a spirit of gentleness" (Galatians 6:1), yet it also requires an overseer to be "above reproach" (1 Timothy 3:2). True restoration is proven by confession, accountability, and the fruit of a changed life over time — not by a prophetic declaration. Pray for genuine repentance, for healing for those who were hurt, and for the church to hold fast to both grace and truth.
