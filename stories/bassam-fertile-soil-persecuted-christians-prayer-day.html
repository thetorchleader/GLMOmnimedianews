Disowned, Beaten, Jailed — and Still Sharing Jesus: A Voice for the Persecuted Church on Its Day of Prayer

By GLM Omnimedia News November 1, 2026 Source: The Christian Post

Today is the International Day of Prayer for the Persecuted Church, observed each year on the first Sunday of November. This year's observance, promoted by The Voice of the Martyrs, centers on a believer named Bassam, a Christian living on the Arabian Peninsula whose decision to follow Jesus cost him nearly everything.

According to The Christian Post, Bassam was disowned by his father, beaten by his wife's brothers, and thrown in jail after leaving Islam and sharing his faith. His wife expelled him from their apartment and rarely allows him to see his daughters. Yet he continues to tell others about Christ, and one of his daughters has now become a follower of Jesus.

"For many years, I was spiritually asleep. I didn't know the truth of who God is. But once I found Christ, I could not stay silent," Bassam said in a video testimony prepared for the day of prayer. "My brothers-in-law beat me often, but I will not turn back to Islam. I gave a Bible to my sister, and one of my daughters now walks with Christ. No matter what happens to me, I will tell people about Jesus."

Todd Nettleton, vice president of message for The Voice of the Martyrs USA, told The Christian Post that the video is meant to give American Christians a clear picture of the people they are praying for. "He has given up everything and yet says, 'I can't stop talking about Jesus,'" Nettleton said. "Those are the kind of men and women that we're praying for this weekend and hopefully praying for throughout the year."

Churches mark the day in different ways, Nettleton said. Some weave prayers for persecuted believers into their regular Sunday services; others set aside the whole day for remembrance. The Voice of the Martyrs has released free resources for churches, families, and small groups, including Bassam's video testimony, prayer guides, and materials for pastors, along with a 24-hour prayer event on social media sharing a new prayer request each hour.

Nettleton also pointed to what he called fertile soil for the Gospel in places where people have grown disillusioned. He cited reports of Muslims encountering Christ through dreams or visions, and recalled an interview with two Iranian Christians who described large numbers of Iranians no longer identifying as Muslim after more than 40 years under the Islamic Republic. "They don't like what they see," he said. "And so they are frustrated with Islam. They are looking for something else."

The report also highlights India, where 13 states have anti-conversion laws that Nettleton said can make ordinary Christian outreach difficult to navigate. The Voice of the Martyrs' current radio program features an Indian pastor who was arrested under one of those laws and repeatedly beaten in prison, yet still found opportunities to share the Gospel with fellow prisoners. State leaders present the laws as protections against forced conversion, but Nettleton argued their broad wording can treat ordinary church activities — even serving coffee before a service — as inducements to convert.

For believers wondering whether prayer from thousands of miles away makes any difference, Nettleton recalled a pastor from Vietnam who told him, "When you pray for Vietnam, you are working right alongside us in Vietnam." The Holy Spirit, Nettleton said, is not bound by space and time, and Christians can stand with suffering believers in Vietnam, China, the Middle East, Nigeria, and elsewhere through prayer. "Don't ever underestimate the power of our prayer to support and encourage and lift up our brothers and sisters," he said.

Source: The Christian Post.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

Bassam's refusal to stay silent, even after losing nearly everything, echoes the apostles who said, "we cannot but speak the things which we have seen and heard" (Acts 4:20). Scripture also commands believers to "remember them that are in bonds, as bound with them" (Hebrews 13:3). Prayer is not a small thing: it is how Christians who live in freedom stand beside brothers and sisters who pay a price for the same faith.
