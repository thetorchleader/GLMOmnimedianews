G3 Ministries Quietly Rebrands as Lumena Ministries Months After Announcing It Was Shutting Down

By GLM Omnimedia News November 19, 2026 Source: Protestia

Reports of the death of G3 Ministries were greatly exaggerated. Three months after the Reformed ministry announced it was concluding operations in the wake of a scandal, Georgia corporate records obtained by Protestia show that the organization never dissolved at all — it simply changed its name and carried on.

According to Protestia, which says it reviewed the state filings, G3 Ministries for the Church Inc. changed its name to Lumena Ministries Inc. on October 28, 2026. It is the same nonprofit corporation, with the same corporate registration number and the same legal history — only the name, the officers, and the address are different.

The story begins in May 2025, when G3 founder and president Josh Buice resigned after admitting he had operated anonymous online accounts used to criticize fellow pastors and ministry leaders, including some of his own associates. Scott Aniol, who had served as the ministry's executive vice president and editor-in-chief of G3 Press, was appointed president that July, and the reorganized ministry promised a renewed direction.

That direction lasted about a year. In August 2026, Protestia reported that leaders associated with G3 and Pray's Mill Baptist Church had been involved in preparing and anonymously distributing allegations of sermon plagiarism against Tom Buck, pastor of First Baptist Church Lindale and a former G3 board member. According to Protestia's reporting, the anonymous accusation packets were sent with misleading return-address labels, and the men involved later acknowledged their part in the campaign and issued an apology letter to Buck's church. Following the reporting, Pray's Mill elders Scott Aniol, Jon Norton, Dylan Joyner, and Matt Sikes resigned from their pastoral positions.

On August 25, G3 told its affiliated churches that the G3 Church Network was dissolving immediately. Soon after, G3's website announced that the broader ministry was concluding its operations, and news outlets widely reported that the organization was shutting down. Yet subscriptions to the G3+ app reportedly continued to be charged.

But the rebrand was already underway. On August 27, just two days after the church-network shutdown announcement, G3 director of operations Laramie Minga filed paperwork with the Georgia Secretary of State establishing a new business, Lumena Ministries LLC, and that same day three new internet domains were registered: lumenaministries.com, lumenapress.com, and lumenamedia.com, according to domain records examined by Protestia.

On September 17, Minga filed to change the name of Lumena Ministries LLC to Minga & Co., LLC — a move Protestia says freed up the Lumena name so that G3 Ministries could take it for itself on October 28. Two days later, on October 30, Minga filed an amended annual registration replacing G3's officers: Minga became CEO, Brad Pogue became CFO, and Trey Etheridge became secretary. Minga also replaced Aniol as the corporation's registered agent, and the address was changed from Pray's Mill Baptist Church to Ramah Baptist Church, where Minga serves alongside pastor Charles Huckaby.

Most remarkably, according to Protestia, the newly installed Lumena board recently sent an email to G3's former supporters announcing that the ministry they had known for thirteen years had “come to an end” and introducing Lumena as a new organization with a “completely new” board — without mentioning that the board is now running the very same nonprofit corporation. The letter is signed by Huckaby as chairman, along with Etheridge, Pogue, and Russell Ross. Protestia notes that while the claim about the directors' previous roles may be literally accurate, the email does not mention that both Huckaby and Minga are former students of Aniol, and that the renamed ministry continues to sell Aniol's books and materials.

Protestia also reports that, according to accounts from Pray's Mill Baptist Church, the church's deacons had determined that Aniol, Sikes, Joyner, and Norton had disqualified themselves from the pastorate — but that Huckaby later met with the deacons to argue that the four former elders had not disqualified themselves, paving the way for them to return to pastoral ministry.

The episode is a sobering reminder for every Christian ministry: a name change is not repentance, and a rebrand is not restoration. True restoration begins with plain truth-telling, open confession, and accountability before God and before the people who gave, trusted, and served. Supporters who were told a ministry had ended deserve to know the whole story — and the church deserves leaders who will tell it without leaving out the parts that matter most.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

The Scripture says, &ldquo;He that covereth his sins shall not prosper: but whoso confesseth and forsaketh them shall have mercy&rdquo; (Proverbs 28:13). A ministry that announces its end while quietly continuing under a new name has not dealt honestly with its supporters. Restoration in the church is never accomplished by a rebrand — it comes only through truth, repentance, and accountability before God.
