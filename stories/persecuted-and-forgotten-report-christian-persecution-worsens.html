New Report: Persecution of Christians Is Worsening Around the World

By GLM Omnimedia News November 24, 2026 Source: Daily Mail

Christians around the world are facing growing violence and persecution, according to a new report from the Catholic charity Aid to the Church in Need (ACN). The report, titled Persecuted and Forgotten? 2024-26, found that conditions for Christians got worse in more than half of the 18 countries it examined, and in none of the countries studied did conditions improve.

The charity estimates that about 220 million Christians worldwide are directly exposed to persecution because of their faith. Separate figures from the Pew Research Center show that Christians faced harassment, from verbal abuse to physical attacks and killings, in 165 countries in 2023, more countries than any other religious group.

Africa has become one of the most dangerous regions for Christians. Six of the ten countries most affected by terrorism in the Global Terrorism Index 2026 are in Africa, including Burkina Faso, Niger, Nigeria, Mali, Somalia and the Democratic Republic of the Congo. In northern Nigeria and across the central Sahel, the report says militants have attacked churches, murdered clergy and driven Christian families from their homes. In Nigeria's Middle Belt, repeated attacks on largely Christian settlements have helped drive a displacement crisis involving more than 3.5 million people, including at least one million Christians.

The report tells the story of Solomon Ako, a 33-year-old Christian from northern Nigeria who was abducted by militants near the city of Zaria. He told ACN that his captors demanded he convert to Islam and, when he refused, beat him with whips and held him and other captives in freezing water up to their necks for hours. The report also describes a June 2025 attack on Yelewata, a town in Nigeria that is about 98 percent Christian, in which more than 200 Christians were killed and around 3,000 people fled. In Mozambique, the report says Islamist militants have killed an estimated 1,000 Christians in Cabo Delgado province since 2017, and in Sudan more than 100 churches have been damaged or destroyed since the civil war began.

Across Asia, the report says, Christians face both violent extremism and state repression. In Pakistan, the report documents the abduction and forced conversion of Christian women and girls, with estimates suggesting as many as 2,000 kidnapping cases a year. One case the report highlights is that of Saba Shafique, a 12-year-old Christian girl from Lahore who was abducted in February 2025 while buying milk at a market; a man is accused of kidnapping her, forcibly converting her and marrying her, and of falsifying documents to make her appear 18. In China, Christians are generally allowed to worship only through state-approved associations, and children under 18 are barred from worship in some provinces. In North Korea, possessing a Bible or taking part in Christian worship can lead to arrest, and believers worship in extreme secrecy. India recorded more than 700 anti-Christian incidents in 2025, according to the report, including attacks on Christmas celebrations and the expulsion of Christian families from their villages.

In the Middle East, the report says Christian minorities continue to face intimidation from Islamist militias and other armed groups, while conflict in Gaza, Lebanon and Syria has displaced Christian communities. In Egypt, the report documents cases of Christian women and girls being abducted and forced to convert and marry. In Iran, the number of Christians arrested rose sharply in 2025 to 254 cases, about double the figure from the year before, and converts from Islam face arrest and imprisonment for apostasy. In June 2026, the report says, security officers entered a church in Tehran during a worship service and warned that the building would be seized.

The findings are a sober reminder that for millions of believers, following Christ carries a real and present cost. The report's authors call on the world not to forget them, and the Church in freer lands is called to remember them in prayer and to speak up on their behalf.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

The suffering Church is not forgotten by God, and it must not be forgotten by us. "Remember them that are in bonds, as bound with them; and them which suffer adversity, as being yourselves also in the body" (Hebrews 13:3). We pray for our brothers and sisters under persecution, for the comfort of those who mourn, and for the hearts of their persecutors to be turned to Christ. "Blessed are they which are persecuted for righteousness' sake: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven" (Matthew 5:10).
