Nine Christians Still Held Captive After Gunmen Seize Worshippers at Kaduna Church Service

By GLM Omnimedia News November 26, 2026 Source: International Christian Concern

Nine Christians remain in captivity after gunmen abducted 12 worshippers during a Sunday church service in Kurmin Wali community, in the Chikun Local Government Area of Kaduna state, Nigeria, according to church leaders.

The Christian Association of Nigeria (CAN), Kaduna state chapter, disclosed the abduction on Tuesday, November 17, two days after the attack on November 15. CAN State Chairman Rev. Caleb Bawa Ma’aji said armed men invaded the community while residents were attending a church service and took 12 worshippers. Three of the victims later escaped and returned to the community, while nine remain with their captors.

Ma’aji also reported that five residents were abducted during another attack on October 5, and that all five are still in captivity. The latest abduction brings the number of people reportedly still held by kidnappers from the two attacks to 14.

The two abductions in Kurmin Wali occurred within six weeks of each other, according to CAN’s account, and the association says repeated attacks and kidnappings have created fear among residents who previously lived peacefully in the community.

Ma’aji criticized the Kaduna State Government’s response to security concerns in the community, saying commitments made by Governor Uba Sani after the October attack had not been fulfilled. He said the governor had promised to establish a security base, construct roads, build a school and provide medical facilities for residents.

The CAN chairman called on the state government to take urgent action to secure the release of the 14 captives. He also urged security agencies to intensify rescue operations and ensure the victims return safely to their families.

Kaduna State Police Command spokesperson DSP Mansir Hassan could not be reached for comment, according to International Christian Concern. He did not respond to telephone calls seeking confirmation of the November 15 attack or information about measures being taken to rescue the abducted worshippers.

No ransom demand has been reported, and no rescue has been announced. The families of the captives wait, and the church in Nigeria asks the wider body of Christ to pray for their safe return.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

When believers are taken captive, the Church’s first duty is prayer. “Remember them that are in bonds, as bound with them; and them which suffer adversity, as being yourselves also in the body” (Hebrews 13:3). Pray for the safe release of these nine worshippers and the five others still held, for comfort for their families, and for the leaders and security forces charged with bringing them home. “The Lord is nigh unto them that are of a broken heart; and saveth such as be of a contrite spirit” (Psalm 34:18).
