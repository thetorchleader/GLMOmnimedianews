Pope Leo XIV Returns to Chiclayo: 'All Christians Are Called to Be Missionary Disciples'

By GLM Omnimedia News November 14, 2026 Source: Catholic World Report

About 1.5 million people turned out to welcome Pope Leo XIV home on Friday, November 13, as he returned to Chiclayo, Peru, the diocese he led for eight years before his election as pope. The pontiff celebrated an open-air Mass on an arid plain outside the city, at the Pampas de Pimentel, where the crowds had gathered in numbers the Vatican put at roughly one million lining his route and another 500,000 attending the liturgy itself.

Leo spent two decades as a missionary in Peru, eight of those years as bishop of Chiclayo. According to the report, he is the first foreign missionary in at least centuries to be elected to the papacy. His return to Chiclayo came a week into a 12-day journey through South America, and the welcome he received made clear how deeply the people of his former diocese still hold him.

Opening his homily, the pope told the crowd, "What a joy it is to return to Chiclayo! It is a blessing for me to be with you once again." He also thanked the people for their prayers during a recent trial of his health, noting that he had undergone cancer surgery only a month earlier and saying that, with God's help, he is overcoming it.

The heart of his message was a call to mission for every believer, not only for priests and religious. "All Christians, whether ordained ministers or lay faithful — regardless of the degree of formation we have received in the faith — are missionary disciples," he said. For most lay Christians, he explained, evangelization takes the form of "the witness of an authentically Christian and coherent life."

That kind of life, the pope said, is not simply a matter of human willpower. It is a grace to be prayed for, and one believers must cooperate with through personal commitment and effort. He tied the proclamation of the faith directly to good works, teaching that evangelization is linked to charity and to care for those most in need.

Leo also warned that a warm welcome for the Gospel must never become an excuse to soften its demands. Jesus, he said, invites believers to conform their lives to his in faithful obedience to his commandments. He closed the thought with a line that framed the whole homily: "Without truth, love degenerates into mere sentimentality, while truth without love becomes rigid and cold."

The visit to Chiclayo followed his return days earlier to Zaña, the small farming town where his ministry as a bishop began, and it stands as a reminder of how much of the Church's life is carried by ordinary believers in ordinary places. A missionary who spent twenty years among the people of northern Peru now speaks to the whole Church from Rome — and his message to his old flock was the same one he carries to the world: every Christian is sent.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

The Great Commission was given to the whole Church, not only to the clergy. "Go ye therefore, and teach all nations" (Matthew 28:19). A pope formed by twenty years of missionary service reminds us that our daily witness — a coherent life, charity toward the needy, truth spoken in love — is itself the mission field God has given us.
