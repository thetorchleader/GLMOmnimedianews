Forrest Frank Warns Followers: 'Do Not Mess Around With God' — Defends the Family, Life, and God's Design

By GLM Omnimedia News November 5, 2026 Source: ChurchLeaders

Christian singer Forrest Frank says he knew speaking up might cost him followers — and he spoke up anyway.

In a video posted to social media the day after the midterm elections, the Dove Award-winning artist warned his followers about what he described as a growing attack on the family. Frank, who has more than 6.9 million Instagram followers, said he had opened his phone to a feed full of posts celebrating new gender identities, transgender procedures for children, and abortion.

“I’m probably gonna lose so many followers for this, and I try to stay out of politics,” Frank said in the video. “But sometimes I feel like it’s important to speak up.”

According to ChurchLeaders, Frank described posts he had seen that boasted about inventing “new genders you haven’t even imagined yet” and about transitioning children, along with a post joking about paying for abortions. He said the messages troubled him deeply.

“You don’t want to mess around with this,” Frank said. “Do not mess around with God. Do not mess around with the family.”

Frank then turned to Scripture, quoting Malachi 4:6: “He will turn the hearts of the fathers to the children, and the hearts of the children to the fathers. Otherwise, I will destroy the land.”

“If we lose the family, we lose everything,” he added.

Frank closed by encouraging Christians to respond not with anger, but by strengthening their own faith and taking responsibility for their families. His message was simple: in a culture that treats the family as something to be reinvented or discarded, believers are called to guard it — beginning in their own homes.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

Frank's warning echoes Malachi 4:6 — God ties the health of a land to the hearts of fathers and children turned toward one another. When the family is treated as disposable, everything built on it is at risk. The faithful response is not fear or fury, but to strengthen our own homes and keep the Lord at the center of them.
