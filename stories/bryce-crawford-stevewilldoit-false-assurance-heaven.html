Street Evangelist Bryce Crawford Tells SteveWillDoIt That No Amount of Money Buys Heaven

By GLM Omnimedia News October 20, 2026 Source: Protestia

Street evangelist Bryce Crawford has once again turned an ordinary podcast conversation into a gospel moment — this time with one of social media's biggest stars, SteveWillDoIt.

Crawford, 22, first rose to prominence filming and posting his street-evangelism encounters, and he now reaches millions through The Bryce Crawford Podcast, which has hosted guests ranging from Howie Mandel and Tucker Carlson to JD Vance and Nicki Minaj. According to reporting by Protestia, Crawford reports roughly 3.3 million followers on Instagram, 4.5 million on TikTok, 2.1 million on YouTube, and another 615,000 on Facebook, with his digital ministry reaching more than 2 billion people since 2024.

His guest in a recent episode was Stephen DeLeonardis — better known online as SteveWillDoIt — a content creator with tens of millions of followers, famous for his association with the NELK Boys, extreme stunts, gambling content, and lavish giveaways.

During the conversation, Crawford pressed the question every evangelist eventually asks: do you believe you are going to heaven? DeLeonardis, in tears, said he was 100 percent convinced he would be — pointing to how much money he gives away and how selfless he tries to be.

Crawford gently but firmly corrected him. No amount of money, he explained, can buy a person's way into heaven.

The exchange cuts against one of the most common and most comfortable misunderstandings of Christianity in America today: that generosity and good deeds are the currency of salvation. DeLeonardis is hardly the first person to believe his giving earns God's favor — but the Bible has always said otherwise.

"For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: not of works, lest any man should boast" (Ephesians 2:8–9, KJV). The apostle Paul is explicit: if salvation could be earned, grace would cease to be grace.

That does not mean giving is worthless. Scripture commends generosity as the fruit of a changed heart — "God loveth a cheerful giver" (2 Corinthians 9:7). But a cheerful giver and a justified sinner are two different things. One flows from the other; it never produces it.

What makes the moment worth noting is not the celebrity of the guest but the seriousness of the conversation. A young evangelist did not flatter a wealthy influencer to win him over. He did what faithful evangelists have done for two thousand years: he asked about eternity, heard a false hope confessed, and pointed — lovingly — to the only true one.

Whether the seed takes root in DeLeonardis's heart, only time and God will tell. But Crawford's approach is a model for the whole Church: love people enough to tell them the truth, especially the truth they do not want to hear. As Jesus Himself said, "I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me" (John 14:6).

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

The Bible warns that works cannot save: "For by grace are ye saved through faith; and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God: not of works, lest any man should boast" (Ephesians 2:8–9). Generosity is a beautiful fruit of a changed heart, but it is never the root of salvation. The only way to heaven is through Jesus Christ — "no man cometh unto the Father, but by me" (John 14:6). Love demands that we tell people this truth, especially when they are counting on the wrong things.
