Israel Faces Pressure on Two Fronts as Trump Weighs Iran Decision and Young Evangelicals Drift

By GLM Omnimedia News November 20, 2026 Source: CBN News

JERUSALEM — Israel is under pressure from two directions at once, CBN News reports: the threat of another confrontation with Iran, and signs that support among young American evangelicals is weakening. The pressure comes as President Donald Trump weighs his next move against the regime in Tehran.

The President told Axios he will hold off until January before deciding whether to order another military strike on Iran. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, by contrast, is pressing for action now. The New York Times reports that Netanyahu is seeking a campaign lasting four to seven days.

In Washington, the U.S. Senate voted 51 to 48 to reject a resolution that would have blocked the President from ordering further strikes. American officials have also cautioned U.S. citizens in the Middle East about the risk of attacks from Iran and from the Houthis.

Meanwhile in Washington, Michigan Senator-elect Abdul El-Sayed has renewed his criticism of Israel and the United States, charging that America aided a genocide — a claim he has made before, and one that Israel and its supporters firmly reject. El-Sayed has also called Netanyahu a war criminal, an accusation that has not been established in any court, and he argues that Israel should no longer define itself by its Jewish and biblical identity.

In Jerusalem, the deeper worry may be the next generation. Bishop Robert Stearns, founder of the Eagles' Wings ministry, is urging Israel to build a long-term strategy to strengthen its ties with young Christians in America before that support fades further.

Hananya Naftali, a former social media adviser to Netanyahu, told CBN News that young people are absorbing a one-sided picture of the conflict from the screens in their hands — a steady stream of images and claims portraying Israel as the aggressor. He said many pastors are afraid even to raise the subject of Israel from the pulpit, and he warned that the young generation's sense of justice on this issue is being formed by media culture rather than by the Word of God.

For now, support for Israel remains strong among older Republican voters. A new poll found that 68 percent of Republican voters in four early 2028 primary states view Israel favorably, compared with 17 percent who view it unfavorably. The question before the Church is whether the next generation will hold the same convictions — and whether pastors will teach them what Scripture says about Israel and the Jewish people.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

“Pray for the peace of Jerusalem: they shall prosper that love thee” (Psalm 122:6). God’s promise to Abraham still stands — “I will bless them that bless thee” (Genesis 12:3) — and the Church must teach the next generation to love Israel from the Scriptures, not from the headlines. When the media sets the story, young hearts are led astray; when the Word sets the story, they are led to truth.
