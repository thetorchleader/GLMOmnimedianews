Kids, Aunties, and Uncles Are Building Websites in East Palo Alto

By GLM Omnimedia News November 23, 2026 Source: Christianity Today

In East Palo Alto, California, children sit down after school to learn how to build websites. So do their aunties, their uncles, and their grandmothers. The classroom is StreetCode Academy, a nonprofit that has spent more than a decade teaching its neighbors the technology skills of the place they call home.

Artist and author Sho Baraka recently traveled to East Palo Alto to visit the academy for a short documentary from Christianity Today's Big Tent Initiative. In the film, Baraka sits down with StreetCode cofounder and chief executive Olatunde Sobomehin to talk about the city, the divide that runs through it, and how faith shapes the academy's work.

East Palo Alto sits in the middle of Silicon Valley, within reach of some of the largest technology companies in the world. Yet for many families there, the innovation economy has felt like something happening somewhere else. Sobomehin told the Mercury News that the academy grew out of a simple observation: too many communities were surrounded by opportunity yet cut off from it. "The idea behind starting the organization was to address the disconnect between certain communities and the innovation opportunities around Silicon Valley," he said. "The community that most embodied that disconnect was right in the heart of Silicon Valley."

StreetCode Academy was founded in 2014 with a mission to empower historically under-resourced communities to reach their full potential by introducing the mindsets, skills, and access needed to take part in the innovation economy. Its courses are free, and they run in four tracks: coding, design, entrepreneurship, and foundational computer skills. Each session ends with a "Demo Day," where students present the projects they have built.

The scale of the work is striking. Since its founding, the academy has served around 9,000 students of all ages across the Bay Area, distributed more than 2,700 devices to families, and offered over 120 free courses. Stanford's Graduate School of Education, where Sobomehin serves as a 2025 Distinguished Practitioner in Residence, reports that StreetCode now serves more than 3,000 students a year and delivers over 50,000 hours of free instruction.

The academy also works to put the tools themselves into people's hands. Its Level Up initiative raised $2.5 million to fund 2,500 laptops, along with internet service and technology education for local students. In December 2025, StreetCode joined AT&T and the office of State Senator Josh Becker to hand out 200 free laptops to East Palo Alto families at a community event. Sobomehin said the pandemic had shown that access is not a short-term fix but "the foundation for opportunity."

The work has drawn recognition. In 2021, Senator Becker named StreetCode Academy the Nonprofit of the Year for his district, praising the organization for redoubling its efforts during the pandemic, when the digital divide threatened to become a chasm for vulnerable families and people of color.

What sets the academy apart is not only what it teaches but how it teaches. Students learn by doing, building real projects and presenting them to their community. The model treats the neighborhood not as a problem to be fixed but as a place full of ideas, creativity, and innovation that the wider tech world has overlooked.

For Christians watching from the outside, the story is a reminder that ministry does not always wear a collar or stand behind a pulpit. Sometimes it looks like a neighbor teaching a neighbor to write code, a grandmother building her first website, or a child discovering that the future being built a few miles away can include her too.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

The people of East Palo Alto had a mind to work, and God blessed it. "And whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men" (Colossians 3:23). When the church and its neighbors serve a community with real skills and real love, the wall gets built — and the next generation learns that their gifts belong in the future God is making.
