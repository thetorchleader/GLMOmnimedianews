Wes Huff Takes Website Offline as Plagiarism Accusations Widen

By GLM Omnimedia News November 15, 2026 Source: Protestia

Christian apologist and historian Wes Huff has taken his website offline amid growing accusations that he plagiarized the work of others, according to a report by Protestia.

Huff serves as the Central Canada Director for Apologetics Canada and is known for his public debates and lectures defending the Christian faith. Earlier this week, biblical scholar Dan McClellan accused Huff of plagiarism, pointing to academic papers Huff posted to Academia.edu in 2016.

Huff responded with a brief video in which he took responsibility for the quality of those early papers. “Their use of citation and attribution is problematic. They’re not well written. They’re all pretty bad,” he said. He later unlisted the video and then deleted it, though Protestia reports it has archived a copy.

Since then, more people have come forward with accusations about content on Huff’s website, saying some of it appeared to be unattributed and suffered from the same problems as the old papers. All of the content was removed from the site, leaving only links to his social media accounts, before the site went offline altogether. His Facebook page was also deleted.

Some of the newer accusations center on infographics on the site that carried Huff’s watermark. Social media users alleged that at least one graphic copied paragraphs word for word from other webpages, including material from an institute and a travel blog, without quotation marks or credit.

Those claims about the graphics remain allegations, not established findings. As Protestia itself noted, a watermark does not prove Huff personally designed or created a graphic — another person or company could have made it for him — and he may have taken the website down in order to review and audit its content.

Huff is also dealing with a medical emergency involving his young daughter, which has limited his ability to address the situation fully. Protestia said it would pray for the child’s recovery and wait for Huff to speak further.

For the church, the episode is a sober reminder that a public ministry built on defending the truth must be matched by truthfulness in the work itself. A reputation for careful, honest scholarship takes years to build and can be shaken in days — and the right response is neither to rush to judgment nor to look the other way, but to deal plainly with what is true and extend grace where repentance is real.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

&ldquo;Study to shew thyself approved unto God, a workman that needeth not to be ashamed, rightly dividing the word of truth&rdquo; (2 Timothy 2:15). The Christian apologist must handle the truth with clean hands &mdash; and when he falls short, honesty and humility are the only road back.
