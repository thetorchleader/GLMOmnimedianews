After 17 Years in Prison, a Father Stayed Connected to His Daughters Through the Church

By GLM Omnimedia News November 21, 2026 Source: ChurchLeaders

George Carter spent seventeen years in prison, separated from his two daughters. He could make phone calls, and they could visit sometimes, but he missed the school days, the family dinners, the milestones, and the everyday moments that shape a child's life. He often looked at photographs of his girls and wondered how much they had changed since the last time he had seen them.

Carter does not hide from the reason he was away. His choices led to the circumstances his family faced, and he took responsibility for that. But he still desired to be a present father. He wanted his children to know he loved them, even though they were apart. He longed to protect them and help them walk through difficult decisions. So he did what he could from a distance.

While behind bars, he found a way to stay connected with his daughters at a time that was especially lonely. He signed them up for Prison Fellowship Angel Tree at Christmastime. Volunteers from a local church purchased and delivered gifts to his girls on his behalf, along with a personal note from him.

One of his daughters later described how she would imagine him shopping and picking out a present just for her. Of course, he could not do that in prison. But in his little girl's mind, her daddy was still thinking of her. While he could not walk through a store or watch them open gifts on Christmas morning, his children knew he was thinking of them and making sure they got something special. He could still be their dad, even from afar.

Looking back, Carter said he realizes how many people helped carry his family when he could not be there with them. His daughters' mother worked hard to preserve the family bonds. Prison Fellowship staff and local church members offered encouragement and support, so he was not the only person invested in his daughters' well-being. These small acts, he said, helped make his family whole again.

Through the kindness of others, Carter said he saw a tangible expression of God's grace. They reminded him that he is more than his past mistakes. God was still at work in his life, and he could still be the good father God created him to be.

He cannot get back the years he missed with his daughters, but he says he is grateful for the memories they are making now. Today his daughters are grown and married, and he is a grandfather. The family shares moments he once only imagined, from playing basketball every Monday with his youngest daughter to filming her future husband's beach proposal.

Almost three million children across the United States have a parent in jail or prison, according to the Sentencing Project. They did not commit a crime, but they are serving a sentence of their own. Children of incarcerated parents often struggle under an inheritance of isolation and shame, and compared to their peers, they may face higher rates of poverty, mental health struggles, and involvement with the justice system.

For those children, a gift at Christmas with a note from a father they cannot see may seem like a small thing. But for George Carter's daughters, it was proof that their daddy was still there — and for their father, it was proof that the church had not forgotten him either.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

“A father of the fatherless, and a judge of the widows, is God in his holy habitation” (Psalm 68:5). When a father cannot be present, the church can stand in the gap — carrying gifts, encouragement, and the love of Christ to children who did nothing wrong. Pray for the nearly three million children in America with a parent behind bars, and for the ministries and local churches that remind them they are not forgotten.
