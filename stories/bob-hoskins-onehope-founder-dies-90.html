Bob Hoskins, Founder of OneHope, Dies at 90 After a Life of Putting God's Word in Children's Hands

By GLM Omnimedia News November 18, 2026 Source: ChurchLeaders

Bob Hoskins, an evangelist and missionary whose vision to put God's Word into the hands of every child helped launch the global ministry OneHope, died November 14 at his home in Florida following a brief battle with cancer. He was 90.

Hoskins devoted more than five decades to bringing the Bible to children and young people around the world, building a ministry that, according to OneHope, has now reached more than 2 billion children through Scripture-based publications and other media.

Born Bobby D. Hoskins on April 15, 1936, in Oklahoma, he began preaching as a child evangelist, traveling throughout the United States. His conviction that God's Word has the power to transform lives eventually took him into missionary work across Africa, Asia, the Middle East, Europe, and South America.

In 1980, Hoskins became president of Life Publishers, the Assemblies of God's international literature ministry. Under his leadership, the organization expanded into a multilingual publishing operation, producing Bibles and ministry resources in Spanish, Portuguese, and French. The work also helped him recognize the potential of combining Scripture with media to reach new audiences.

Seven years later, Hoskins said he received a clear conviction from God: he was to help bring the message of the Bible to every child and young person in the world. An opportunity to begin that work came in 1988, when El Salvador's minister of education requested Bibles for the country's nearly 1 million schoolchildren.

Hoskins recognized that a traditional Bible format might not effectively engage young readers, so he developed a culturally relevant, illustrated publication that presented Scripture in an accessible, magazine-style format. The result was the first Book of Hope, which reached nearly 1 million children in El Salvador. The initiative became the foundation for Book of Hope International, later renamed OneHope.

What began as a project in one Central American nation grew into an international ministry with a mission to give every child and young person an opportunity to encounter God's Word. Today, OneHope continues that work through a variety of media, including animated films such as The GodMan and interactive resources such as the Bible App for Kids. The organization says its Scripture-based publications have reached more than 2 billion children and young people worldwide.

"Bob Hoskins's legacy is both profound and lasting," said Stephan Tchividjian, chairman of the OneHope Board of Directors. "He fully embraced the Lord's vision to reach every child in every generation with the message of Jesus through His Word, drawing from his deep dependence on and personal understanding of Scripture."

Tchividjian described Hoskins as a selfless leader who prioritized serving the global church, inviting others into the mission and modeling "faithfulness, courage, and joy." He added, "While we may never fully know the impact of his obedience, his example compels the OneHope community to carry this God-given vision forward until every child has the opportunity to hear about Jesus."

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

Bob Hoskins believed one thing with his whole life: a child who meets God's Word can be changed forever. His ministry began with one country's schoolchildren and grew to reach billions, because he trusted the promise of Scripture: 'Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it' (Proverbs 22:6, KJV). Every believer can take up the same calling in their own home, church, and neighborhood — put God's Word into a child's hands.
