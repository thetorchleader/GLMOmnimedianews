Jesus Calling: Not Recommended for the PCA, Yet Given Freely to Inmates and Women in Recovery

By GLM Omnimedia News November 15, 2026 Source: Protestia

The Presbyterian Church in America does not recommend Jesus Calling for its own members. But for thirteen years, the Jesus Calling Foundation has been putting the book into the hands of prison inmates and women in addiction recovery — people far more vulnerable than the church members the denomination sought to protect.

That is the heart of a new report from Protestia, published November 14, which draws on the foundation's own public tax filings and on the Presbyterian Church in America's (PCA) 2025 investigation of the bestselling devotional by the late Sarah Young.

For over a decade, the Jesus Calling Foundation has provided custom paperback copies of the book to The Next Door Recovery, a Nashville-area ministry that gives the books to prison inmates and to every woman who enters its residential and transitional care facilities. In a promotional video for the ministry, staff members describe the book as central to their recovery program. “Each woman who arrives at The Next Door receives a copy of Jesus Calling and is encouraged to read the daily passages as a way to rekindle their faith and establish a daily connection to God while they're in recovery,” the staff explain. “Jesus Calling is foundational to who we are and it's a beautiful tool.”

Meanwhile, the foundation's financial support for The Next Door has grown sharply. According to its publicly filed Form 990, donations to the ministry rose from $50,000 to $212,500 in 2025, presumably providing even more books to women outside the PCA. Next Door Executive Director Rachel Morris announced the arrival of the new books with enthusiasm: “Today is Jesus Calling delivery day!”

The contrast with the denomination's own position is stark. In 2025, as part of its investigation into Jesus Calling, the PCA required its Committee on Discipleship Ministries to assess whether the book was appropriate “for Christians in general and PCA members and congregations in particular.” The committee reported that it did not recommend Jesus Calling for PCA congregants, but it declined to comment on the book's suitability for other Christians, much less as an evangelistic tool.

Protestia, in a guest piece by Dr. Benjamin Inman, argues that the denomination's silence amounts to a tacit blessing of the book's use in evangelism. “They actually are going to say this book is a Christian book, just not a very good one — not good enough for people like them,” Inman said ahead of the 2025 report. The piece contends that the PCA, having judged the book unfit for its own members, has a duty to say so plainly to the ministries and the vulnerable people who receive it.

For context, Jesus Calling, first published in 2004, has sold more than 45 million copies, making it one of the most widely read devotionals in modern church life. It has also been one of the most debated, with critics raising concerns about its first-person “voice of Jesus” format and its treatment of Scripture's sufficiency. Sarah Young was a longtime PCA member whose husband is an ordained PCA minister, and she served as a missionary under the denomination's Mission to the World agency.

It is important to keep the facts straight. The PCA's decision not to recommend the book is an established fact, documented in the denomination's own reporting. The dollar figures come from the foundation's publicly filed tax returns. But the charge that the denomination is effectively endorsing the book by its silence is Protestia's judgment, not a finding of any church court — and the article reports no response from the PCA or from The Next Door to that characterization.

What is not in dispute is that the book continues to reach the people with the least power to question it: men and women behind bars, and women rebuilding their lives after addiction. Whatever a church body concludes about a book, the weakest among us deserve the clearest teaching, not the teaching a denomination has already set aside for itself.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

The Lord Jesus warned His shepherds about the little ones: “Whoever causes one of these little ones who believe in me to sin, it would be better for him to have a great millstone fastened around his neck and to be drowned in the depth of the sea” (Matthew 18:6). If a book is not sound enough for a denomination's own members, it is not sound enough for prisoners and women in recovery either. The vulnerable deserve the pure Word of God, not a second-tier substitute.
