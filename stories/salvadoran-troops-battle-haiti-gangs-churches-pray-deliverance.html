Haiti's Churches Pray for Deliverance as Salvadoran Troops Take On the Gangs

By GLM Omnimedia News November 23, 2026 Source: CBN News

In Haiti, where armed gangs rule much of daily life, a new force has entered the fight — and the nation's churches are answering with prayer. El Salvador has sent 2,000 soldiers to the Caribbean nation after four Salvadoran airmen serving with a United Nations security force disappeared in a gang ambush, CBN News reports.

Many Haitians are cheering the troops on. Since landing in late October on a search mission, the Salvadoran forces have, according to President Nayib Bukele, destroyed the headquarters of the 400 Mawozo gang and helped retake the national stadium in Port-au-Prince. Hundreds of Haitians have marched in several cities to thank Bukele for the help.

But religious leaders in Haiti say soldiers alone cannot rebuild a broken nation. The country has had no elected government of its own choosing in years — Haiti has held no elections in a decade — and its last president, Jovenel Moïse, was assassinated in 2021. Gangs moved into the vacuum his death left behind, and they now rule the crowded slum districts that sprang up outside the capital after the 2010 earthquake.

The human cost is staggering. Brian Concannon of the Institute for Justice and Democracy in Haiti told CBN that Haiti is one of the hungriest countries in the world and one of the worst places to get health care, with some 1.5 million people displaced by the violence. He cautioned that military pressure alone may only push the problem elsewhere, saying the gangs "mostly just go somewhere else" when pressed — "squeezing a balloon."

In one of the poorest areas, a church is praying around the clock. Pastor Esdras Jean Pierre of Life Source Church in Canaan described living under constant threat: "They are living close to us. They are surrounding us. Sometimes they come to our church to take the church, to take our school, to take our house."

Ken Isaacs of Samaritan's Purse, which still runs an orphanage and a school in Haiti with only a few staff left, said most Americans do not grasp the depth of the evil at work there. In his view, the darkness has a spiritual root, and he believes the country is held in bondage by forces that only God can break.

The corruption runs deep, according to those who know the country best. In an earlier CBN interview, the notorious gang leader Lanmò Sanjou — now being hunted by the Salvadoran forces — said the people running Haiti are funding and facilitating the very gangs they claim to want rid of. Isaacs echoed the charge, saying, "Politicians bring weapons in, they organize the gangs, they equip the gangs, they turn them against each other." Concannon added that the world has poured roughly a billion dollars a year into Haiti for thirty years, yet conditions are far worse than when the aid began — and he argued the international community must do what it did in the 1990s: work toward an accountable, elected government.

Pastor Esdras hopes this moment is a first step toward defeating the gangs and holding free elections. "We need someone to lead the Haitian people to the light, because they are in darkness, in deep darkness, and they don't know what's happening to them exactly. We need prayer." At last report, the four missing Salvadoran airmen had still not been found, and Bukele speaks of finishing Haiti's gangs the way he did back home. Whatever the soldiers achieve, the pastors of Haiti are clear about what the nation needs most: deliverance that only God can give, and the prayers of God's people until it comes.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

Haiti's pastors are showing the Church what to do when the powers of this world fail: pray without ceasing. "The righteous cry, and the LORD heareth, and delivereth them out of all their troubles" (Psalm 34:17). No army, no aid program, and no election can break the bondage of a nation in darkness — but the light of Christ can. "If my people, which are called by my name, shall humble themselves, and pray, and seek my face, and turn from their wicked ways; then will I hear from heaven, and will forgive their sin, and will heal their land" (2 Chronicles 7:14). Let us join the church in Canaan in praying for Haiti's deliverance.
