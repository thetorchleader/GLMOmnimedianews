Nashville Church Says Fallen Pastor Steven Lawson Is Fit for Ministry Again, Nearly a Year After Quiet Restoration

By GLM Omnimedia News November 11, 2026 Source: ChurchLeaders

Almost one year ago, the elders of Stephens Valley Church in Nashville, Tennessee, decided that fallen pastor Steven Lawson was ready for ministry again. In a document dated November 25, 2025, the church's elder council declared Lawson 'spiritually fit for ministry opportunities that the Lord may open to him,' commending what they called his 'broken and contrite heart,' his service to their congregation, and his 'pursuit of and patience toward his loving wife, Anne.'

The decision closes a long and painful chapter in one of the most closely watched falls from ministry in recent years — and it opens a serious question that faithful Christians answer differently: when a pastor falls into grave sin, can he ever return to the pulpit?

Lawson was removed as lead pastor of Trinity Bible Church of Dallas in September 2024 after confessing to what the church described as 'an inappropriate relationship' with a woman who was not his wife. Lawson, who was 73 at the time, admitted the relationship had lasted five years and involved a woman in her 20s. He had also served as a professor and dean at The Master's Seminary in California, the school founded by John MacArthur. Lawson has been married for more than four decades and has four adult children.

The fall was swift and total. After Lawson confessed, Austin Duncan of The Master's Seminary said Lawson had 'permanently disqualified' himself from pastoral ministry and had 'brought disgrace to the name of Christ.' Lawson was removed from the seminary's website and from the website of OnePassion Ministries, the organization he founded.

In January 2025, Alabama pastor Clint Archer shared an update, saying Lawson had moved from Dallas to Nashville to stay temporarily with his brother and to receive spiritual counseling while Trinity Bible Church and Lawson's family 'processed the shocking news' of the affair. Archer said at the time that Lawson was 'fully aware that he is permanently disqualified from ministry and is not under any illusion that he could ever be restored to a teaching position or ministry capacity that requires the qualifications of an elder.' Archer added that Lawson had returned cash advances for book contracts he would no longer publish and was 'considering avenues for secular employment.'

Lawson broke his public silence in March 2025, admitting that he had 'sinned grievously against the Lord, against my wife, my family, and against countless numbers of you by having a sinful relationship with a woman not my wife.' He said he was 'deeply broken that I have betrayed and deceived my wife, devastated my children, brought shame to the name of Christ, reproach upon His church, and harm to many ministries.' He said he was receiving 'wise counsel' and oversight from an accountability team.

In September 2026, Lawson released a book about his fall titled 'Mercy in the Wilderness: How I Fell & What I Found.' According to Protestia, he also plans to release a book about the life of King David.

The Nashville church's decision to restore Lawson to ministry will not settle the debate. On one side stand those who hold that the biblical qualifications for an elder — above reproach, faithful to his wife — once forfeited by adultery, cannot be regained for pulpit ministry, whatever repentance follows. On the other stand those who point to the restoration of Peter after his denial and to David's own return after his sin with Bathsheba, and who argue that genuine repentance can open the door to service again. Both sides appeal to Scripture, and both agree on this much: the wound Lawson's sin caused his wife, his family, his former congregation, and the name of Christ was real and deep.

What is not in dispute is that Lawson himself has owned his sin publicly and plainly, without excuse. Whether the church at large receives him back into ministry is a question each congregation and ministry will now have to answer for itself.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

The Bible holds mercy and holiness together: 'Brethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness' (Galatians 6:1), yet an elder must also be 'blameless, the husband of one wife' (1 Timothy 3:2). Repentance is always welcome before God, and the church must be careful both to forgive the repentant and to guard the pulpit with the high standard Scripture sets.
