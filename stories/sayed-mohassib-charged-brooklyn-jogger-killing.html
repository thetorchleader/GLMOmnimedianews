Accused Brooklyn Jogger Killer, a Former NYPD Officer and School Aide, Charged With Murder After Reported Confession

By GLM Omnimedia News November 23, 2026 Source: Charisma News (via The Western Journal / New York Post)

BROOKLYN, N.Y. — A 27-year-old former New York City police officer and elementary school aide has been charged with second-degree murder in the killing of a Brooklyn jogger, and he has reportedly told a newspaper that he carried out the attack because he believed God told him to do it.

Sayed Mohassib is accused of killing Katie Suarez, 28, a bank teller who was attacked while jogging on October 30. She was found by her father and died three days later after being placed on life support, according to Charisma News, which republished a report from The Western Journal.

According to the New York Post, Mohassib said, “Allah told me to kill a person, so I killed her, the jogger Katie Suarez.” He added, “I didn’t know her, she was just there,” and said the instruction had come to him two days before the attack. He also told the Post that he had at first denied involvement, but now says he did it because he believed he was told to.

“I got home from work, I got the knife, and I killed her… I went home, I cleaned up,” he reportedly said. Asked whether he had a message for Suarez’s grieving family, he replied, “No. Allah told me to do it.”

Mohassib is charged with second-degree murder. He has not been convicted, and the case against him is pending. His reported statements to the newspaper are claims he made to a reporter, not findings of a court.

According to the report, Mohassib worked for the New York Police Department for six weeks in 2022. He enlisted in the Marines but was discharged and classified as psychologically unfit. He later worked as a paraprofessional at a New York City elementary school. The New York Post noted that his family had at one time alleged he threatened to “chop them up.”

His mother, Fatma Mohassib, defended her son, telling WABC-TV, “He’s a black belt, he goes to Boy Scouts all his life, goes to the mosque all his life, he goes to Brooklyn College, the team basketball, excellent boy.”

The killing of an innocent young woman out for a run has shaken her family and her Brooklyn neighborhood, and it raises hard questions about how a man with a troubled history came to work around small children in a public school. Those questions now belong to investigators, prosecutors, and the courts, and they deserve careful, honest answers.

For the Christian, one thing must be said plainly: the God of the Bible never commands the murder of an innocent stranger. “Thou shalt not kill” (Exodus 20:13) is not a suggestion, and no voice urging a man to take a life he has no right to take is the voice of the Lord. When a person claims God told him to do evil, the claim itself stands condemned by the Word of God.

Pray for the family of Katie Suarez, who have lost a daughter in the most senseless way. Pray also for truth and justice in this case, and for a young man whose life, by every account, has been marked by deep trouble — that he would come to know the true God, who sent His Son not to destroy men’s lives, but to save them.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

God never commands murder: “Thou shalt not kill” (Exodus 20:13). Any voice urging a man to take an innocent life is not the voice of the Lord, for “whosoever hateth his brother is a murderer” (1 John 3:15). Pray for Katie Suarez’s grieving family, for truth and justice in this case, and for the true God to be known.
