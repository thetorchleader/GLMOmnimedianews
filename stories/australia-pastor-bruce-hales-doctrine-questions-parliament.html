Australian Lawmakers' Questions About Church Doctrine Raise Religious Liberty Concerns

By GLM Omnimedia News October 25, 2026 Source: Charisma Magazine Online

An Australian parliamentary hearing that began as an inquiry into election activity has raised a warning flag for religious liberty advocates after lawmakers turned their questions toward the doctrines and daily life of a Christian church.

According to Charisma Magazine Online, reporting on coverage by Fox News Digital, Bruce Hales, the global leader of the Plymouth Brethren Christian Church, appeared before an Australian parliamentary committee on October 16. The committee was investigating allegations surrounding political activity by church members during Australia's 2025 federal election.

The hearing soon moved beyond campaign questions. Independent Member of Parliament Monique Ryan asked Hales whether church members regard him as an 'elect vessel' or a messenger from God, and whether members seek his guidance on personal matters such as housing, marriage, facial hair, clothing, and even owning pets.

Not every lawmaker on the committee was comfortable with that line of questioning. Liberal MP Ben Small pushed back, describing it as outside the committee's mandate, according to the report.

Hales has denied funding, coordinating, or directing political campaigns by church members.

Pastor Lucas Miles, vice president of faith at Turning Point USA, told Fox News Digital that American Christians should be paying close attention. He argued that while a government has every right to investigate violations of election law, it has no business putting Christian doctrine on trial.

Miles has reportedly contacted the White House Faith Office and members of the Trump administration, urging President Donald Trump to raise the matter with Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese. He described the case as a warning sign for religious liberty around the world.

The episode raises a question that reaches far beyond one hearing room: where does legitimate oversight of political activity end, and where does government inquiry into what Christians believe begin? That boundary matters for every church, because the freedom to hold and practice doctrine without state interrogation is at the heart of religious liberty.

Christians in Australia and elsewhere will be watching to see whether the committee returns its focus to election law, or whether questions about belief and church life continue to take center stage.

Biblical Worldview Takeaway

When believers are called to answer for their faith before earthly authorities, Scripture gives both courage and clarity: 'We ought to obey God rather than men' (Acts 5:29). Christians can pray for leaders, respect lawful authority, and still hold firmly that what the church believes and how it orders its life belong to God, not to any government committee.
